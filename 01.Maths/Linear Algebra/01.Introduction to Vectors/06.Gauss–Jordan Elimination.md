**Gauss–Jordan Elimination** is a method for solving linear systems by transforming a matrix all the way into **Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)**.

Think of it as:
- Gaussian Elimination + one extra step
- Gaussian Elimination clears entries **below** pivots.
- Gauss–Jordan Elimination clears entries **below and above** pivots.

The final matrix is so simple that the solution can be read directly. Transform the augmented matrix $[A\mid b]$ into $[I\mid x]$ where:
- $I$ is the identity matrix
- $x$ is the solution vector
For example, $\begin{bmatrix}   1&0&|&2\\  0&1&|&3  \end{bmatrix}$ immediately tells us $x=2,\quad y=3$.

## Allowed Row Operations

| Operation                            | Notation                  |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Swap two rows                        | $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$ |
| Multiply a row by a nonzero number   | $R_i \leftarrow cR_i$     |
| Add a multiple of one row to another | $R_i \leftarrow R_i+kR_j$ |

The third operation is used most often because it lets us create zeros.


For each pivot:
1. Make the pivot equal to 1.
2. Eliminate everything below it.
3. Eliminate everything above it.
    The pivot column should eventually look like $\begin{pmatrix}  0\\  1\\  0  \end{pmatrix}$with the pivot as the only nonzero entry.

---

## Steps of Gauss–Jordan Elimination

### Step 1: Write the Augmented Matrix

Convert the system into matrix form.

Example:

$x+2y=5$

$3x+4y=6$

becomes

$\begin{bmatrix}  
1&2&|&5\  
3&4&|&6  
\end{bmatrix}$

---

### Step 2: Create the First Pivot

Make the first pivot equal to 1 if necessary.

A pivot is the first nonzero entry in a row.

---

### Step 3: Eliminate Below the Pivot

Use row operations to make all entries below the pivot equal to 0.

This is the same step used in Gaussian Elimination.

---

### Step 4: Move to the Next Pivot

Repeat the process in the next column.

Continue until all pivots have been found.

---

### Step 5: Eliminate Above the Pivots

Now work upward.

Make all entries above each pivot equal to 0.

This is the extra step that converts REF into RREF.

---

## Example

**Start with:**
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 & | & 5 \\
3 & 4 & | & 6
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Eliminate below the first pivot** -> $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3R_1$:
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 & | & 5 \\
0 & -2 & | & -9
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Make the second pivot equal to 1** -> $R_2 \leftarrow -\frac{1}{2}R_2$:
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 2 & | & 5 \\
0 & 1 & | & 4.5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Eliminate above the second pivot** -> $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 2R_2$:
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & | & -4 \\
0 & 1 & | & 4.5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

This matrix is now in **RREF**. Read the solution directly:

$$x = -4, \qquad y = 4.5$$

---

## Why is Gauss–Jordan Useful?

It directly reveals:
- the solution of a system
- pivot variables
- free variables
- rank
- null space

No back-substitution is needed.

---

## Gaussian Elimination vs Gauss–Jordan Elimination

| Method                   | Final Form             | Need Back Substitution? |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Gaussian Elimination     | REF (upper triangular) | Yes                     |
| Gauss–Jordan Elimination | RREF                   | No                      |

Example REF: $\begin{bmatrix}  1&2&3\\  0&1&4\\   0&0&1  \end{bmatrix}$
Example RREF: $\begin{bmatrix}  1&0&0\\  0&1&0\\  0&0&1  \end{bmatrix}$


>[!note] Gaussian Elimination clears below pivots. Gauss–Jordan Elimination clears below **and above** pivots.
> **Gaussian = Staircase.**
> **Gauss–Jordan = Identity Matrix.**
