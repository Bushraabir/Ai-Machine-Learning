
Suppose we already know $A^{-1}$.

Now we slightly modify the matrix:

$(A+\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T)$

where $\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T$ is a **rank-1 matrix**.

Instead of recomputing the inverse from scratch, Sherman-Morrison lets us update the old inverse:
$(A+\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T)^{-1} A^{-1}\dfrac{A^{-1}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^TA^{-1}}  {1+\mathbf{v}^TA^{-1}\mathbf{u}}$

---

### What is a rank-1 update?

Let $\mathbf{u}=  \begin{pmatrix}  1\\  2  \end{pmatrix},  \qquad  \mathbf{v}=  \begin{pmatrix}  3\\  4  \end{pmatrix}$.
Then $\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T \begin{pmatrix}  1\\  2  \end{pmatrix}  \begin{pmatrix}  3 & 4  \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}  3 & 4\\  6 & 8  \end{pmatrix}$.
Notice that Row 2 is just $2\times$ Row 1, so there is only one independent direction.

Therefore; $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T)=1$.

---
### Matrix Picture
Start with $A=  \begin{pmatrix}  2 & 0\\  0 & 2  \end{pmatrix}$.
Then $A+\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T \begin{pmatrix}   2 & 0\\  0 & 2  \end{pmatrix}+  \begin{pmatrix}  3 & 4\\  6 & 8  \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}  5 & 4\\  6 & 10  \end{pmatrix}$.

We changed every entry, but the change came from only **one new direction** (rank 1).

Sherman-Morrison says:

> Don't throw away $A^{-1}$ and start over. Update it using a correction term.

---

Think of the formula as:
**New inverse = Old inverse − Correction**

You do not need to memorize every symbol. Remember the idea:
> Small change to the matrix ⇒ small update to the inverse.

---

### Why is it useful?

Recomputing an inverse costs: $O(n^3)$
Updating using Sherman-Morrison costs: $O(n^2)$

Much cheaper when matrices are large.
