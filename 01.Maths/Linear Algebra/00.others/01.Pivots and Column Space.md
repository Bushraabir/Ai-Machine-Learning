When you row-reduce a matrix, some columns "earn" a **pivot** — the first nonzero entry in a row that wasn't zeroed out. The columns that contain pivots are the _pivot columns_, and they span the **column space** of $A$.

> [!tldr] One-Line Intuition Pivots mark the **linearly independent columns**. Everything in the column space is a linear combination of exactly those columns.

---

## 1. What Is a Pivot?

After reducing $A$ to row echelon form (REF):

$$ A \xrightarrow{\text{row ops}} U $$

A **pivot** is the **leading nonzero entry** in each nonzero row of $U$.

| Term               | Definition                                                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pivot position** | Location $(i,j)$ of a pivot in $U$                                             |
| **Pivot column**   | Column $j$ of $A$ (original!) that contains a pivot                            |
| **Free column**    | Column with no pivot — can be expressed as a combo of pivot columns            |
| **Pivot variable** | Variable corresponding to a pivot column                                       |
| **Free variable**  | Variable corresponding to a free column — set freely to parameterize solutions |

> [!warning] Common Mistake Pivot columns refer to **columns of $A$**, not of $U$. Row operations preserve which columns are independent, but change the actual vectors — always trace pivots back to $A$.

---

## 2. Row Reduction and Identifying Pivots

```python
import numpy as np

A = np.array([
    [1, 2, 0, 3],
    [2, 4, 1, 7],
    [3, 6, 1, 10]
], dtype=float)

# Row reduce manually: R2 -= 2*R1, R3 -= 3*R1
# Then R3 -= R2
# Result (REF):
# [1  2  0  3]
# [0  0  1  1]
# [0  0  0  0]
# Pivots at positions (row 0, col 0) and (row 1, col 2)
# Pivot columns: 0 and 2  →  free columns: 1 and 3
```

The rank of $A$ equals the number of pivots: $r = 2$ here.

## 3. Column Space — Formal Definition

The **column space** (also called the **range**) of $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ is:

$$ \text{Col}(A) = { A\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n } \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m $$

Equivalently, it is the **span of the columns** of $A$:

$$ \text{Col}(A) = \text{span}{\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_n} $$

> [!note] Why It Matters $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has a solution **if and only if** $\mathbf{b} \in \text{Col}(A)$.  
> The column space is precisely the set of all right-hand sides $\mathbf{b}$ for which the system is consistent.

---

## 4. Pivot Columns as a Basis for Col(A)

> [!theorem] Key Theorem The **pivot columns of $A$** (original, not reduced) form a **basis** for $\text{Col}(A)$.

**Why?**

- **Spanning:** Every free column is a linear combination of the pivot columns (read off from the reduced form).
- **Independence:** Pivot columns are linearly independent by construction — no pivot column can be written as a combination of the _previous_ ones.

### Example

From the matrix above: $$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 1 & 7 \\ 3 & 6 & 1 & 10 \end{bmatrix} $$

Pivot columns: **columns 1 and 3** (1-indexed), i.e., $\mathbf{a}_1 = \begin{bmatrix}1\\2\\3\end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{a}_3 = \begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{bmatrix}$.
$$
\operatorname{Col}(A)
=
\operatorname{span}\left\{
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\
2\\
3
\end{bmatrix},
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
1\\
1
\end{bmatrix}
\right\}
\subset \mathbb{R}^3
$$
This is a **2-dimensional subspace** of $\mathbb{R}^3$ — a plane through the origin.

---

## 5. Rank = Dimension of Column Space

$$ \text{rank}(A) = \dim(\text{Col}(A)) = \text{number of pivots} $$

|Matrix property|Meaning|
|---|---|
|$\text{rank}(A) = m$|**Full row rank** — every $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^m$ is reachable; system always consistent|
|$\text{rank}(A) = n$|**Full column rank** — columns are independent; at most one solution|
|$\text{rank}(A) = m = n$|$A$ is **invertible**; unique solution for every $\mathbf{b}$|

---

## 6. Rank-Nullity Theorem (Bridge to Null Space)

$$ \underbrace{\text{rank}(A)}_{\dim \text{Col}(A)} + \underbrace{\text{nullity}(A)}_{\dim \text{Null}(A)} = n $$

- **Pivot columns** $\rightarrow$ basis of $\text{Col}(A)$, dimension $= r$
- **Free columns** $\rightarrow$ number of free variables $=$ $\dim(\text{Null}(A))$ $= n - r$

> [!example] Quick Check For a $5 \times 7$ matrix with rank 3:
> 
> - $\dim(\text{Col}(A)) = 3$ (lives in $\mathbb{R}^5$)
> - $\dim(\text{Null}(A)) = 7 - 3 = 4$ (lives in $\mathbb{R}^7$)

---

## 7. Column Space vs Row Space

|-|Column Space $\text{Col}(A)$|Row Space $\text{Row}(A)$|
|---|---|---|
|**Subspace of**|$\mathbb{R}^m$|$\mathbb{R}^n$|
|**Basis from**|Pivot columns of **original** $A$|Nonzero rows of **reduced** $U$|
|**Dimension**|$\text{rank}(A)$|$\text{rank}(A)$ (same!)|
|**Geometric meaning**|All reachable outputs $A\mathbf{x}$|All directions input space "sees"|

Both have dimension equal to rank — this is a deep symmetry of linear maps.

## 8. Worked Example End-to-End

$$ B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 6 & 4 \\ 1 & 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix} $$

**Step 1: Row reduce**
$$ \xrightarrow{R_2 - 2R_1,\\ R_3 - R_1} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \xrightarrow{R_3 - \frac{1}{2}R_2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

**Step 2: Identify pivots**

- Pivot at $(1,1)$ → column 1 is a pivot column
- Pivot at $(2,3)$ → column 3 is a pivot column
- Column 2 is free

**Step 3: Basis for Col($B$)**

$$
\operatorname{Col}(B)
=
\operatorname{span}\left\{
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\
2\\
1
\end{bmatrix},
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\
4\\
2
\end{bmatrix}
\right\}
$$
(Take from original $B$, not reduced form.)

**Step 4: Express free column in terms of pivot columns**

Column 2 of $B$: $\begin{bmatrix}3\\6\\3\end{bmatrix} = 3\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\\1\end{bmatrix} + 0\begin{bmatrix}1\\4\\2\end{bmatrix}$
