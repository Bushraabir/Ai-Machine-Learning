For a square, invertible matrix, we can solve $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ using $\mathbf{x}=A^{-1}\mathbf{b}$.

But many matrices are **not invertible**:

- The matrix may be rectangular (more rows than columns or vice versa)
    
- The matrix may be singular (determinant (=0))
    
- The system may have no exact solution
    

The **Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse**, denoted by $A^+$, acts like the "best possible inverse" even when a true inverse does not exist.

Instead of

$\mathbf{x}=A^{-1}\mathbf{b}$,

we use

$\mathbf{x}=A^+\mathbf{b}$.

---

### Why do we need it?

Consider

$A=  
\begin{pmatrix}  
1 & 0\  
0 & 1\  
1 & 1  
\end{pmatrix}$.

This matrix is (3\times2), so it is **not square**.

Since only square matrices can have ordinary inverses, $A^{-1}$ does not exist.

However, we can still compute $A^+$ and use it to find the "best" solution to

$A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$.

---

### Matrix Picture

Suppose we want to solve

$A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$

with

$A=  
\begin{pmatrix}  
1 & 0\  
0 & 1\  
1 & 1  
\end{pmatrix},  
\qquad  
\mathbf{b}=  
\begin{pmatrix}  
1\  
2\  
10  
\end{pmatrix}$.

There is no exact solution because the third equation would require

$x+y=10$,

while the first two equations give

$x=1,\qquad y=2$

and therefore

$x+y=3$.

The system is inconsistent.

The pseudoinverse chooses the vector $\mathbf{x}$ that makes

$A\mathbf{x}$

as close as possible to $\mathbf{b}$.

---

### Geometric Meaning

Think of the columns of $A$ as defining a subspace called the **column space**.

Not every vector $\mathbf{b}$ lies in that subspace.

The pseudoinverse does two things:

1. Projects $\mathbf{b}$ onto the column space of $A$
    
2. Finds the vector $\mathbf{x}$ that produces that projection
    

So instead of solving

$A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$,

it solves

> "Which vector in the column space is closest to $\mathbf{b}$?"

---

### Mental Model

Think of the pseudoinverse as:

**Best Approximate Inverse**

or

**Inverse for matrices that are not invertible.**

You do not need to remember the construction.

Remember the idea:

> When a true inverse does not exist, the pseudoinverse gives the best possible solution.

---

### Special Case

If $A$ is square and invertible, then

$A^+=A^{-1}$.

So the pseudoinverse is a generalization of the ordinary inverse.

---

### Why is it useful?

The pseudoinverse allows us to solve systems that are:

- Overdetermined (more equations than unknowns)
    
- Underdetermined (more unknowns than equations)
    
- Singular
    
- Rectangular
    

It automatically gives the **least-squares solution**, meaning the solution with the smallest error.

---

### One-Sentence Memory Trick

> Ordinary inverse solves exact systems. Pseudoinverse finds the closest possible solution when an exact solution does not exist.
