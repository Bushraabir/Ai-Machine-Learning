> [!note] Why Apply Gauss-Jordan to the Pascal Matrix?
> 
> The Pascal matrix is already known to have $\det = 1$ and a sign-alternating inverse. But **watching Gauss-Jordan work on it teaches you something deeper**:
> 
> - The elimination steps themselves encode **combinatorial identities** — each row operation is a finite difference operation in disguise
> - The pivot sequence (all 1s) confirms positive definiteness and unit determinant without any computation of $\det$
> - The inverse produced by augmented row reduction exactly matches the alternating-sign formula — giving you an **algorithmic proof** of that theorem
> - Every step is exact integer arithmetic: no fractions, no rounding, no partial pivoting needed
> 
> The one insight: **Gauss-Jordan on the Pascal matrix is finite difference calculus expressed as row operations.**

## Setup: Augmented Matrix $[L_n \mid I]$

We apply Gauss-Jordan to the **lower triangular Pascal matrix** $L_n$ to compute $L_n^{-1}$ directly.

Work with $n = 4$:

$$L_4 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \ 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \ 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \ 1 & 3 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Augment with $I_4$:

$$[L_4 \mid I_4] = \left[\begin{array}{cccc|cccc} 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \ 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \ 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \ 1 & 3 & 3 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right]$$

**Goal:** Apply row operations until the left block becomes $I_4$. The right block will then be $L_4^{-1}$.

> [!note] Why No Partial Pivoting Is Needed
> 
> $L_4$ is lower triangular with all diagonal entries equal to 1. Every pivot is exactly 1 — guaranteed without any row swaps. Gauss-Jordan proceeds cleanly from top to bottom, then bottom to top.

---

## Phase 1: Forward Elimination (Upper Triangular → Lower Zeros)

Since $L_4$ is already lower triangular, **forward elimination clears the entries below each pivot** — but they are already zero. The first phase is trivial.

The matrix is already in **row echelon form** after zero operations.

> [!note] This Is Not Trivial for $S_n$
> 
> For the **symmetric** Pascal matrix $S_4$, forward elimination is nontrivial and more revealing. We will work $S_4$ fully after completing $L_4$.

---

## Phase 2: Back Substitution (Clear Entries Above Each Pivot)

Since all pivots are 1, we clear **above** each pivot column using the pivot row.

**Pivot column 4** (pivot row 4): clear entries in rows 1, 2, 3 above.

$$R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 0 \cdot R_4 \quad (\text{already 0})$$ $$R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 0 \cdot R_4 \quad (\text{already 0})$$ $$R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 0 \cdot R_4 \quad (\text{already 0})$$

Column 4 is already clean. Move to column 3.

**Pivot column 3** (pivot row 3): entry above in row 2 is 0, row 1 is 0. Already clean.

**Pivot column 2** (pivot row 2): entry in row 1 is 0. Already clean.

The left block is already $I_4$ — confirming $L_4$ was already in reduced row echelon form as a lower triangular matrix with unit diagonal.

> [!warning] This Gives the Wrong Insight
> 
> Reading $L_4$ as trivial misses the point. The real computation happens when we apply Gauss-Jordan to **$S_4$**, where the off-diagonal entries are nonzero and the row operations encode Pascal's recurrence. That is the case we fully trace below.

---

## Full Gauss-Jordan on $S_4$: The Revealing Case

$$S_4 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \ 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \ 1 & 3 & 6 & 10 \ 1 & 4 & 10 & 20 \end{bmatrix}$$

Augmented matrix:

$$[S_4 \mid I_4] = \left[\begin{array}{cccc|cccc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \ 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \ 1 & 3 & 6 & 10 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \ 1 & 4 & 10 & 20 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right]$$

---

### Step 1: Eliminate Column 1 Below Pivot (Pivot = $S_{11} = 1$)

Operations: $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 1 \cdot R_1$, $\quad R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 1 \cdot R_1$, $\quad R_4 \leftarrow R_4 - 1 \cdot R_1$

$$\left[\begin{array}{cccc|cccc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & -1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 2 & 5 & 9 & -1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \ 0 & 3 & 9 & 19 & -1 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right]$$

> [!note] Pascal Recurrence Appears
> 
> Look at the new row 2: $[0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \ldots]$ — this is $\binom{j-1}{1}$, exactly the second row of $U_4$. The row operation $R_2 - R_1$ computes $\binom{j-1}{1} - \binom{j-1}{0} = \binom{j-2}{0}$... the **forward difference** of the first row. Gauss-Jordan elimination **is** the finite difference operator $\Delta$ applied row by row.

---

### Step 2: Eliminate Column 2 Below Pivot (Pivot = 1)

Operations: $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 2 \cdot R_2$, $\quad R_4 \leftarrow R_4 - 3 \cdot R_2$

$$R_3: [0,\ 2,\ 5,\ 9\ |\ {-1},\ 0,\ 1,\ 0] - 2 \cdot [0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3\ |\ {-1},\ 1,\ 0,\ 0]$$ $$= [0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 3\ |\ 1,\ {-2},\ 1,\ 0]$$

$$R_4: [0,\ 3,\ 9,\ 19\ |\ {-1},\ 0,\ 0,\ 1] - 3 \cdot [0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3\ |\ {-1},\ 1,\ 0,\ 0]$$ $$= [0,\ 0,\ 3,\ 10\ |\ 2,\ {-3},\ 0,\ 1]$$

$$\left[\begin{array}{cccc|cccc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & -1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 1 & 3 & 1 & -2 & 1 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 3 & 10 & 2 & -3 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right]$$

---

### Step 3: Eliminate Column 3 Below Pivot (Pivot = 1)

Operation: $R_4 \leftarrow R_4 - 3 \cdot R_3$

$$R_4: [0,\ 0,\ 3,\ 10\ |\ 2,\ {-3},\ 0,\ 1] - 3 \cdot [0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 3\ |\ 1,\ {-2},\ 1,\ 0]$$ $$= [0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 1\ |\ {-1},\ 3,\ {-3},\ 1]$$

$$\left[\begin{array}{cccc|cccc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & -1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 1 & 3 & 1 & -2 & 1 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \end{array}\right]$$

> [!note] Forward Elimination Complete
> 
> The left block is now upper triangular with all pivots equal to **1**.
> 
> - All pivots are positive → $S_4$ is **positive definite** ✓
> - Product of pivots = $1^4 = 1$ → $\det(S_4) = 1$ ✓
> - No row swaps needed → no sign change in determinant ✓
> 
> The right block is $L_4^{-1}$ taking shape: look at the last row — $[-1,\ 3,\ -3,\ 1]$ — alternating signs of row 4 of $L_4$. The pattern is emerging.

---

### Step 4: Back Substitution — Clear Column 4 Above Pivot

Operations: $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 3 \cdot R_4$, $\quad R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 3 \cdot R_4$, $\quad R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 1 \cdot R_4$

$$R_3 \leftarrow [0,0,1,3\ |\ 1,-2,1,0] - 3[0,0,0,1\ |\ -1,3,-3,1]$$ $$= [0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 0\ |\ 4,\ -5,\ 10-9 ,\ 0-3] = [0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 0\ |\ 4,\ -5,\ -8+9,\ -3]$$

Let me compute cleanly:

$R_3$: entries right of bar: $(1-3(-1),\ -2-3(3),\ 1-3(-3),\ 0-3(1)) = (1+3,\ -2-9,\ 1+9,\ -3) = (4,\ -11,\ 10,\ -3)$

> [!warning] Recomputing carefully
> 
> $R_3 = [0,0,1,3\ |\ 1,-2,1,0]$, $\quad R_4 = [0,0,0,1\ |\ -1,3,-3,1]$
> 
> $R_3 - 3R_4$: left block $[0,0,1,3-3] = [0,0,1,0]$ Right block: $(1-3(-1),\ -2-3(3),\ 1-3(-3),\ 0-3(1)) = (1+3,\ -2-9,\ 1+9,\ -3) = (4,\ -11,\ 10,\ -3)$

Hmm — that doesn't match the alternating pattern. Let me recheck $R_3$ from Step 3.

After Step 3, $R_3 = [0,0,1,3\ |\ 1,-2,1,0]$.

$R_3 - 3R_4 = [0,0,1,0\ |\ 1-3(-1),\ -2-3(3),\ 1-3(-3),\ 0-3(1)]$ $= [0,0,1,0\ |\ 4,\ -11,\ 10,\ -3]$

$R_2 - 3R_4 = [0,1,2,0\ |\ -1-3(-1),\ 1-3(3),\ 0-3(-3),\ 0-3(1)]$ $= [0,1,2,0\ |\ 2,\ -8,\ 9,\ -3]$

$R_1 - 1 \cdot R_4 = [1,1,1,0\ |\ 1-(-1),\ 0-3,\ 0-(-3),\ 0-1]$ $= [1,1,1,0\ |\ 2,\ -3,\ 3,\ -1]$

$$\left[\begin{array}{cccc|cccc} 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 2 & -3 & 3 & -1 \ 0 & 1 & 2 & 0 & 2 & -8 & 9 & -3 \ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 4 & -11 & 10 & -3 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \end{array}\right]$$

---

### Step 5: Back Substitution — Clear Column 3 Above Pivot

Operations: $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2 \cdot R_3$, $\quad R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 1 \cdot R_3$

$R_2 - 2R_3$: left $[0,1,0,0]$, right: $(2-8,\ -8+22,\ 9-20,\ -3+6) = (-6,\ 14,\ -11,\ 3)$

Hmm — let me recheck. $R_2 = [0,1,2,0\ |\ 2,-8,9,-3]$, $R_3 = [0,0,1,0\ |\ 4,-11,10,-3]$.

$R_2 - 2R_3$: right: $(2-2(4),\ -8-2(-11),\ 9-2(10),\ -3-2(-3)) = (2-8,\ -8+22,\ 9-20,\ -3+6) = (-6,\ 14,\ -11,\ 3)$

$R_1 - R_3$: left $[1,1,0,0]$, right: $(2-4,\ -3+11,\ 3-10,\ -1+3) = (-2,\ 8,\ -7,\ 2)$

$$\left[\begin{array}{cccc|cccc} 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & -2 & 8 & -7 & 2 \ 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & -6 & 14 & -11 & 3 \ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 4 & -11 & 10 & -3 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \end{array}\right]$$

---

### Step 6: Back Substitution — Clear Column 2 Above Pivot

Operation: $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 1 \cdot R_2$

$R_1 - R_2$: left $[1,0,0,0]$, right: $(-2-(-6),\ 8-14,\ -7-(-11),\ 2-3) = (4,\ -6,\ 4,\ -1)$

$$\left[\begin{array}{cccc|cccc} 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 4 & -6 & 4 & -1 \ 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & -6 & 14 & -11 & 3 \ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 4 & -11 & 10 & -3 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \end{array}\right]$$

---

### Result: $S_4^{-1}$

$$\boxed{S_4^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & -6 & 4 & -1 \ -6 & 14 & -11 & 3 \ 4 & -11 & 10 & -3 \ -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}$$

---

## Verification

### Check 1: $S_4 S_4^{-1} = I$

Row 1 of $S_4$ times column 1 of $S_4^{-1}$: $$[1,1,1,1] \cdot [4,-6,4,-1]^T = 4 - 6 + 4 - 1 = 1 \checkmark$$

Row 1 of $S_4$ times column 2 of $S_4^{-1}$: $$[1,1,1,1] \cdot [-6,14,-11,3]^T = -6 + 14 - 11 + 3 = 0 \checkmark$$

Row 2 of $S_4$ times column 1 of $S_4^{-1}$: $$[1,2,3,4] \cdot [4,-6,4,-1]^T = 4 - 12 + 12 - 4 = 0 \checkmark$$

Row 4 of $S_4$ times column 4 of $S_4^{-1}$: $$[1,4,10,20] \cdot [-1,3,-3,1]^T = -1 + 12 - 30 + 20 = 1 \checkmark$$

### Check 2: Alternating Sign Pattern

The formula predicts $[S_n^{-1}]_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j} [S_n]_{ij}$.

$$\text{Expected: } (-1)^{i+j}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \ 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \ 1 & 3 & 6 & 10 \ 1 & 4 & 10 & 20 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & -6 & 4 & -1 \ -6 & 14 & -11 & 3 \ 4 & -11 & 10 & -3 \ -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Wait — the $(1,1)$ entry should be $(-1)^2 \cdot 1 = 1$, but we got 4. The formula $(-1)^{i+j}[S_n]_{ij}$ applies only when $[S_n]_{ij}$ are the **original** entries. Let's verify:

$(-1)^{1+1}[S]_{11} = 1$, but $[S^{-1}]_{11} = 4$. These differ — the inverse is **not** simply the signed version of the original entries for $S_4$.

> [!warning] Clarification: The Alternating Sign Formula
> 
> The sign-alternating inverse $[M^{-1}]_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j}[M]_{ij}$ holds for $L_n$ and $U_n$ (the triangular Pascal matrices), **not** for $S_n = L_n L_n^T$ directly.
> 
> For $S_n$, the inverse is $S_n^{-1} = (L_n^T)^{-1} L_n^{-1} = (L_n^{-1})^T L_n^{-1}$, which produces different entries.
> 
> The Gauss-Jordan computation above gives the **correct** $S_4^{-1}$. The sign-alternating shortcut applies only to the triangular factors.

### Check 3: Symmetry of the Inverse

$S_4^{-1}$ is clearly symmetric (rows and columns match) — as expected, since $S_4$ is symmetric and $(S^{-1})^T = (S^T)^{-1} = S^{-1}$. ✓

### Check 4: Determinant via Pivots

All four pivots during Gauss-Jordan were $1$. Therefore: $$\det(S_4) = 1 \times 1 \times 1 \times 1 = 1 \checkmark$$

## What the Row Operations Reveal

> [!theorem] Row Operations on $S_n$ Are Finite Difference Operations
> 
> At Step 1, subtracting row 1 from each subsequent row transforms row $i$ of $S_n$: $$[S_n]_{i,\cdot} - [S_n]_{1,\cdot}: \quad \binom{i+j-2}{i-1} - \binom{j-1}{0} = \binom{i+j-2}{i-1} - 1$$
> 
> More precisely, the elimination multiplier for row $i$ against pivot row 1 is $\binom{i-1}{1} = i-1$... actually the multiplier is $[S_n]_{i,1}/[S_n]_{1,1} = \binom{i-1}{0} = 1$ for all $i$.
> 
> Each subtraction $R_i \leftarrow R_i - R_1$ computes the **first forward difference** of the sequence $\binom{i+j-2}{i-1}$ in the row index, producing $\binom{i+j-3}{i-2}$ — the entry one row higher in $S_{n-1}$. Elimination peels away one layer of Pascal's Triangle.

|After eliminating column|Submatrix structure of remaining block|
|---|---|
|Column 1 eliminated|Rows/cols $2\ldots n$: copy of $S_{n-1}$|
|Column 2 eliminated|Rows/cols $3\ldots n$: copy of $S_{n-2}$|
|Column $k$ eliminated|Rows/cols $k+1\ldots n$: copy of $S_{n-k}$|

> [!note] The Recursive Pascal Structure Under Elimination
> 
> Each forward elimination step reveals $S_{n-1}$ inside $S_n$. This is the matrix-algebraic version of the Pascal recurrence $\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k-1} + \binom{n-1}{k}$: eliminating the first row/column of $S_n$ leaves exactly $S_{n-1}$.
> 
> This is why all pivots are 1 and the determinant is always 1: $\det(S_n) = \text{pivot}_1 \times \det(S_{n-1}) = 1 \times 1 \times \cdots \times 1$.

## Pivot Sequence and Positive Definiteness Proof

|Step|Pivot|Submatrix|Conclusion|
|---|---|---|---|
|$k=1$|$s_{11} = 1 > 0$|$S_4$|Leading $1\times 1$ minor $> 0$|
|$k=2$|Schur complement $= 1 > 0$|$S_3$ revealed|Leading $2\times 2$ minor $> 0$|
|$k=3$|Schur complement $= 1 > 0$|$S_2$ revealed|Leading $3\times 3$ minor $> 0$|
|$k=4$|Schur complement $= 1 > 0$|$S_1 = [1]$|Leading $4\times 4$ minor $> 0$|

All leading principal minors are positive → $S_4$ is **positive definite** (Sylvester's criterion).

> [!theorem] Sylvester's Criterion Applied
> 
> A symmetric matrix $A$ is positive definite **if and only if** all leading principal minors are strictly positive.
> 
> For $S_n$: every leading principal minor $\det(S_k) = 1 > 0$ for $k = 1, \ldots, n$. Gauss-Jordan simultaneously computes the inverse **and** certifies positive definiteness via Sylvester's criterion.

> [!tip] Decision Rule
> 
> - Need $S_n^{-1}$ symbolically? → Use $(L_n^{-1})^T L_n^{-1}$ with the sign-alternating formula for $L_n^{-1}$
> - Need $L_n^{-1}$ only? → Negate off-diagonal entries (no row reduction needed)
> - Want to **understand** what elimination does to Pascal structure? → Trace Gauss-Jordan on $S_n$ as above
> - Want to verify positive definiteness? → Attempt Cholesky (faster) or read pivots from GJ
> - Numerical computation for large $n$? → Use `scipy.linalg.pascal` + `numpy.linalg.inv`, but expect ill-conditioning

## Python Verification

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import pascal

n = 4
S = pascal(n, kind='symmetric').astype(float)

# Gauss-Jordan via numpy
S_inv = np.linalg.inv(S)

print("S_inv =")
print(np.round(S_inv).astype(int))
# Expected:
# [[ 4  -6   4  -1]
#  [-6  14 -11   3]
#  [ 4 -11  10  -3]
#  [-1   3  -3   1]]

# Verify S @ S_inv = I
print("S @ S_inv =")
print(np.round(S @ S_inv).astype(int))

# Verify determinant
print("det(S) =", round(np.linalg.det(S)))  # Should be 1

# Verify all pivots = 1 via Cholesky (since S = L L^T, all pivots are 1)
L = np.linalg.cholesky(S)
print("Cholesky diagonal (= sqrt of pivots):", np.round(np.diag(L)).astype(int))
# All 1s
```
