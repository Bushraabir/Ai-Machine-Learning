
An **echelon matrix** is the result of applying row operations to $A$ until it reaches a standardized "staircase" shape. This shape makes the structure of the linear system immediately readable — you can see the rank, pivots, free variables, and solution set at a glance.

> [!tldr] One-Line Intuition Echelon form is what a matrix looks like when you've done _just enough_ work to read off all the information. RREF goes one step further — it makes the solution explicit.

---

## 1. The Two Forms

There are two levels of echelon form. Both are reached by **elementary row operations** only:

|Operation|Notation|Effect|
|---|---|---|
|Swap two rows|$R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$|Reorder rows|
|Scale a row|$R_i \leftarrow c R_i$|Multiply every entry by $c \neq 0$|
|Add multiple of one row to another|$R_i \leftarrow R_i + c R_j$|Eliminate entries|

> [!warning] Key Constraint Row operations **preserve the solution set** of $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ and preserve the **row space** of $A$. They do **not** preserve the column space — that's why pivot columns must be traced back to the original $A$.

---

## 2. Row Echelon Form (REF)

A matrix is in **Row Echelon Form** if:

1. All zero rows are at the **bottom**
2. Each **leading entry** (pivot) in a nonzero row is to the **right** of the leading entry in the row above
3. All entries **below** each pivot are **zero**

$$ U = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{2} & 5 & -1 & 3 \ 0 & \boxed{3} & 4 & -2 \ 0 & 0 & \boxed{1} & 7 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

The pivots (boxed) form the **staircase**. Each step goes right by at least one column.

> [!note] REF is Not Unique Different sequences of row operations can produce different REFs for the same matrix. The **pivot positions** are always the same, but the pivot values and entries above them may differ.

---

## 3. Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)

RREF adds two more conditions on top of REF:

4. Each pivot is exactly **1** (scale the row)
5. All entries **above** each pivot are also **zero** (back-substitution)

$$ R = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{1} & 0 & 0 & 2 \ 0 & \boxed{1} & 0 & -3 \ 0 & 0 & \boxed{1} & 5 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

> [!theorem] Uniqueness of RREF Every matrix has **exactly one** RREF. This is a theorem — unlike REF, RREF is a unique canonical form. It encodes the full structure of the linear map.

---

## 4. REF → RREF: The Full Algorithm

**Forward elimination** (REF):

```
for each column j from left to right:
    find a nonzero entry in column j at or below current row i  (pivot)
    swap that row to position i
    eliminate all entries below the pivot using row ops
    move to next row i+1
```

**Back substitution** (REF → RREF):

```
for each pivot row from bottom to top:
    scale row so pivot = 1
    eliminate all entries ABOVE the pivot
```

### Full Worked Example

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 & 4 & 2 \ 1 & 3 & 5 & 7 \ 2 & 2 & 2 & 6 \end{bmatrix} $$

**Step 1:** Swap $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$ (need nonzero in column 1, row 1)

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 5 & 7 \ 0 & 2 & 4 & 2 \ 2 & 2 & 2 & 6 \end{bmatrix} $$

**Step 2:** $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 2R_1$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 5 & 7 \ 0 & 2 & 4 & 2 \ 0 & -4 & -8 & -8 \end{bmatrix} $$

**Step 3:** $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 + 2R_2$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 5 & 7 \ 0 & 2 & 4 & 2 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & -4 \end{bmatrix} \quad \leftarrow \text{REF reached} $$

Pivots at columns **1, 2, 4**. Column 3 is free.

**Step 4:** Scale pivot rows so each pivot $= 1$

$R_2 \leftarrow \frac{1}{2}R_2$, $R_3 \leftarrow -\frac{1}{4}R_3$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 5 & 7 \ 0 & 1 & 2 & 1 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

**Step 5:** Eliminate above pivot in column 4: $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - R_3$, $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 7R_3$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 5 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 2 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

**Step 6:** Eliminate above pivot in column 2: $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - 3R_2$

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 2 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \quad \leftarrow \text{RREF} $$

## 5. Reading Information Directly from RREF

Once in RREF, everything is explicit:

|What you want to know|How to read it from RREF|
|---|---|
|**Rank**|Count the pivots (= nonzero rows)|
|**Pivot columns**|Columns containing a leading 1|
|**Free variables**|Variables for non-pivot columns — assign parameters $t_1, t_2, \ldots$|
|**Consistency**|Inconsistent iff a row $[0\ 0\ \cdots\ 0\ \mid\ c]$ with $c \neq 0$ appears|
|**Unique solution**|Consistent AND $r = n$ (no free variables)|
|**Infinitely many**|Consistent AND $r < n$ (at least one free variable)|

### Reading the Solution from RREF Above

From $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 2 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, augmented system $[R \mid \mathbf{0}]$:

- $x_4 = 0$ (row 3)
- $x_3 = t$ (free variable)
- $x_2 = -2t$ (row 2: $x_2 + 2x_3 = 0$)
- $x_1 = t$ (row 1: $x_1 - x_3 = 0$)

$$ \mathbf{x} = t\begin{bmatrix}1\-2\1\0\end{bmatrix}, \quad t \in \mathbb{R} $$

This is the **null space** of $A$ — one-dimensional, as expected from $n - r = 4 - 3 = 1$.

## 6. Comparison Table: REF vs RREF

|Property|REF|RREF|
|---|---|---|
|Zero rows at bottom|✓|✓|
|Staircase pattern|✓|✓|
|Zeros below each pivot|✓|✓|
|Pivot values equal 1|✗|✓|
|Zeros above each pivot|✗|✓|
|Uniqueness|✗ (not unique)|✓ (unique)|
|Sufficient to find rank|✓|✓|
|Sufficient to read solution directly|✗ (need back-sub)|✓|
|Used in Gaussian elimination|✓|✓|
|Used in Gauss-Jordan elimination|—|✓|

## 7. Special Cases

> [!example] Identity Matrix — Perfect RREF $$I_n = \begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\0&1&0\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}$$ Rank $= n$, zero null space. Every column is a pivot column.

> [!example] Zero Matrix — Trivial RREF $$0_{m \times n}$$ Rank $= 0$. Every variable is free. Null space $= \mathbb{R}^n$.

> [!example] Rank-Deficient Square Matrix $$\begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\2&4&6\1&2&3\end{bmatrix} \xrightarrow{\text{RREF}} \begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\0&0&0\0&0&0\end{bmatrix}$$ Rank $= 1$. Columns 2 and 3 are free. The three rows were all multiples of each other — only one independent direction.

## 8. Computational Note

```python
import sympy as sp

A = sp.Matrix([
    [0, 2, 4, 2],
    [1, 3, 5, 7],
    [2, 2, 2, 6]
])

# RREF with pivot column indices
rref, pivot_cols = A.rref()

print("RREF:")
sp.pprint(rref)
print("Pivot columns (0-indexed):", pivot_cols)
# Output: (0, 1, 3)
```

> [!warning] Numerical vs Symbolic `numpy` uses floating point — rounding errors can create "near-zero" pivots that should be exact zeros. Use `sympy` for exact RREF. In ML/data contexts, use `numpy.linalg.matrix_rank` with a tolerance for rank estimation.

## 9. Connection to Four Fundamental Subspaces

The echelon forms are the computational engine behind all four subspaces:

|Subspace|Dimension|How echelon form helps|
|---|---|---|
|$\text{Col}(A)$|$r$|Pivot columns of original $A$|
|$\text{Row}(A)$|$r$|Nonzero rows of REF/RREF|
|$\text{Null}(A)$|$n-r$|Free variables from RREF give basis vectors|
|$\text{Null}(A^T)$|$m-r$|Row reduce $A^T$|

---

## 10. ML Relevance

> [!tip] Why This Matters in ML
> 
> - **Feature preprocessing:** RREF reveals exact linear dependencies in feature matrices — if two features are linearly dependent, their columns reduce to one pivot + one free column. Drop the redundant one.
> - **Solving normal equations:** $X^T X \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}} = X^T \mathbf{y}$ — row reduction on the augmented matrix $[X^TX \mid X^T\mathbf{y}]$ is equivalent to solving least squares directly.
> - **Rank estimation for PCA:** The number of pivots in the data matrix (up to numerical tolerance) equals the intrinsic dimensionality of your dataset.
> - **Checking identifiability:** In statistical models, the design matrix $X$ must have full column rank. RREF immediately exposes multicollinearity.

## Summary

$$ A \xrightarrow{\text{forward elimination}} \underbrace{U}_{\text{REF}} \xrightarrow{\text{back substitution + scaling}} \underbrace{R}_{\text{RREF}} $$

- **REF:** staircase shape, zeros below pivots, pivots can be anything nonzero
- **RREF:** pivots are 1, zeros above AND below pivots, **unique** for every matrix
- Pivot count $=$ rank $=$ dimension of column space $=$ dimension of row space
- Free column count $=$ nullity $= n -$ rank
