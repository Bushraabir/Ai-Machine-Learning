    The Fourier matrix is the matrix representation of the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT).
    It converts a vector from the time / spatial domain into the frequency domain.

    For an integer \(n\), the Fourier matrix \(F \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}\) is defined as

    \[ F = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & \cdots & 1 \\ 1 & \omega & \omega^2 & \cdots & \omega^{n-1} \\ 1 & \omega^2 & \omega^4 & \cdots & \omega^{2(n-1)} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 1 & \omega^{n-1} & \omega^{2(n-1)} & \cdots & \omega^{(n-1)^2} \end{pmatrix} \]

    where

    \[ \omega = e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}} \]

    is a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity.

    Compact Formula¶

    The entry of the Fourier matrix can be written compactly as

    $$ F_{jk} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \omega^{jk} $$ for \(j,k = 0,1,2,\ldots,n-1\) Thus each entry is a power of the complex root of unity.

    Interpretation¶

    Multiplying a vector by the Fourier matrix performs the Discrete Fourier Transform.

    If

    \[ x = \begin{pmatrix} x_0 \\ x_1 \\ \vdots \\ x_{n-1} \end{pmatrix} \]

    then

    \[ y = Fx \]

    produces the frequency representation of \(x\).

    Each component \(y_k\) measures the strength of the frequency \(k\) inside the signal.

    Key Properties¶

    1. Unitary Matrix¶

    The Fourier matrix is unitary, meaning $$ F^F = FF^ = I $$ where \(F^*\) is the conjugate transpose.

    Thus \(F^{-1} = F^*\) This property preserves vector length: $$ |Fx| = |x| $$

    2. Orthogonal Basis (in Complex Space)¶

    The columns of \(F\) form an orthonormal basis of \(\mathbb{C}^n\). Each column represents a complex sinusoidal wave with a different frequency.

    3. Diagonalizes Circulant Matrices¶

    A major theoretical result: If \(C\) is a circulant matrix, then $$ C = F^* \Lambda F $$where \(\Lambda\) is a diagonal matrix of eigenvalues. This means the Fourier matrix diagonalizes all circulant matrices simultaneously.

    Example: \(n = 3\)¶

    For \(n=3\) $$ \omega = e^{2\pi i/3} $$ The Fourier matrix becomes $$ F = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \ 1 & \omega & \omega^2 \ 1 & \omega^2 & \omega \end{pmatrix} $$

    Geometric Meaning¶

    The numbers \(1, \omega, \omega^2, \ldots, \omega^{n-1}\) lie evenly spaced on the unit circle in the complex plane. Thus the Fourier matrix is built from complex rotations, which encode periodic patterns.

    Why the Fourier Matrix Matters¶

    The Fourier matrix is foundational in many areas: - Signal processing - Image compression - Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) algorithms - Solving convolution problems - Diagonalizing circulant matrices - Quantum computing It provides the mathematical bridge between time-domain structure and frequency-domain structure.
