07.Gibbs Sampling

07.Gibbs Sampling

Apr 02, 20266 min read

1. The Core Problem It Solves

You want to sample from a joint distribution p(x1​,x2​,…,xn​) that is:

  • Too complex to sample from directly (no closed form, high-dimensional), or
  • Only known up to a normalizing constant: p(x)∝p~​(x)

Gibbs Sampling sidesteps this by exploiting the fact that even when the joint is intractable, the full conditionals p(xi​∣x∖i​) are often tractable.

Joint: intractable⟹Conditionals: tractable

2. The Algorithm

Setup

  • Variables: x=(x1​,x2​,…,xd​)
  • Full conditional of variable i: p(xi​∣x1​,…,xi−1​,xi+1​,…,xd​), written p(xi​∣x∖i​)

Pseudocode

Initialize: x^(0) = (x_1^(0), ..., x_d^(0))   ← random or prior draw

For t = 1, 2, ..., T:
    Sample x_1^(t)  ~ p(x_1  | x_2^(t-1), x_3^(t-1), ..., x_d^(t-1))
    Sample x_2^(t)  ~ p(x_2  | x_1^(t),   x_3^(t-1), ..., x_d^(t-1))
    Sample x_3^(t)  ~ p(x_3  | x_1^(t),   x_2^(t),   ..., x_d^(t-1))
    ...
    Sample x_d^(t)  ~ p(x_d  | x_1^(t),   x_2^(t),   ..., x_{d-1}^(t))

Return: {x^(t)} after burn-in B steps

Key: Each variable is updated one at a time, using the most recent values of all others (systematic scan).

3. Why It Works — The MCMC Lens

Gibbs sampling is a special case of Metropolis-Hastings with acceptance rate always = 1.

Each update step defines a Markov chain transition:

T(xinew​∣x)=p(xinew​∣x∖i​)

This satisfies detailed balance with respect to the target joint p(x):

p(x)⋅T(x′∣x)=p(x′)⋅T(x∣x′)

Proof sketch (2D case):

p(x1​,x2​)⋅p(x1′​∣x2​)=p(x1​,x2​)⋅p(x2​)p(x1′​,x2​)​ =p(x1′​,x2​)⋅p(x2​)p(x1​,x2​)​=p(x1′​,x2​)⋅p(x1​∣x2​)✓

Therefore: the stationary distribution of the chain is the target joint p(x).

5. Bayesian Inference Use Case — LDA Flavor

In Latent Dirichlet Allocation and Bayesian models, you often need:

p(θ,z∣x)∝p(x∣z)⋅p(z∣θ)⋅p(θ)

Gibbs cycles between:

  1. p(θ∣z,x) — often Dirichlet (conjugate)
  2. p(z∣θ,x) — often Categorical

This is collapsed Gibbs sampling when θ is analytically integrated out, sampling only z.

7. Critical Failure Modes

7.1 Slow Mixing (High Correlation)

If ρ≈1 in a Gaussian, the chain takes tiny correlated steps — ESS collapses.

Fix: Use reparameterization, or switch to Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC).

7.2 Multimodal Distributions

If p(x) has multiple modes separated by low-probability regions, the chain gets trapped in one mode.

Mode A ████████       ░░░░░░░░   Mode B ████████
          ↑ chain stuck here

Fix: Simulated Tempering, Parallel Tempering, or Normalizing Flows.

7.3 Deterministic Scan vs. Random Scan

  • Systematic (deterministic) scan: Update x1​,x2​,…,xd​ in fixed order. Simpler, commonly used.
  • Random scan: Pick xi​ uniformly at random. Easier to prove detailed balance; preferred theoretically.

8. Gibbs vs. Metropolis-Hastings

PropertyGibbsMetropolis-Hastings
Acceptance stepAlways accept (rate = 1)Explicit accept/reject
Proposal distributionFull conditional (exact)Arbitrary (Gaussian, etc.)
Requires full conditionalYes (must be tractable)No
EfficiencyHigh when conditionals are easyFlexible but tuning-heavy
Generalizes toHMC, Collapsed GibbsNUTS, MALA

Insight: Gibbs = MH with a proposal of q(xi′​∣x)=p(xi′​∣x∖i​). The acceptance ratio is always 1.

12. Mental Model / Intuition

Think of Gibbs sampling as coordinate descent for distributions — instead of minimizing a loss function one variable at a time, you’re sampling from the conditional distribution one variable at a time. The stationary point of descent becomes the stationary distribution of the Markov chain.
