04.Adam's Law with Extra Conditioning

04.Adam's Law with Extra Conditioning

Apr 02, 20267 min read

This is the version of Adam’s Law that trips people up on exams and in proofs. The basic form E[E[X∣Y]]=E[X] is intuitive. But what happens when there’s already some conditioning in the background — when everything is happening conditional on Z? The answer is clean, but you have to see it clearly at least once to trust it.

0. The Statement

E[E[X∣Y,Z]∣Z]=E[X∣Z]​

Reading it: inside, we condition on both Y and Z. Outside, we average over Y — but Z is held fixed throughout. The result: the Y-conditioning disappears, and we’re left with conditioning on Z alone.

This is the Tower Property in full generality. The basic Adam’s Law E[E[X∣Y]]=E[X] is the special case Z=∅ (no background conditioning).

1. Proof

Work conditional on Z=z throughout. For fixed z, apply ordinary Adam’s Law in the conditional universe where Z=z:

E[E[X∣Y,Z]∣Z=z]=E[E[X∣Y,Z=z]∣Z=z]

Now Z=z is fixed — so E[X∣Y,Z=z] is a function of Y alone (call it g(Y) where g(y)=E[X∣Y=y,Z=z]). Adam’s Law in the Z=z universe says:

E[g(Y)∣Z=z]=E[X∣Z=z]

Since this holds for every z, we conclude:

E[E[X∣Y,Z]∣Z]=E[X∣Z]■

The proof is just: freeze Z, apply ordinary Adam’s Law in the reduced universe, unfreeze. Every property of conditional expectation works this way — condition on Z and you’re back in familiar territory.

2. The Nesting Condition

The Tower Property requires that the inner conditioning is finer than the outer. Precisely:

E[E[X∣G]∣H]=E[X∣H]when H⊆G

In variable terms: the outer conditioning must involve less information than the inner. Examples of valid nestings:

InnerOuterValid?
E[X∣Y,Z]condition on Z✓ — Z alone is less than (Y,Z)
E[X∣Y,Z]condition on Y✓ — Y alone is less than (Y,Z)
E[X∣Y]condition on g(Y) for some function g✓ — g(Y) is coarser than Y
E[X∣Y]condition on Z (unrelated)✗ — no nesting, cannot simplify

The last row is the common mistake. E[E[X∣Y]∣Z] does not generally simplify unless Z and Y are related (e.g. Z is a function of Y, or X⊥Z∣Y).

3. Two Directions of the Tower

There are two useful forms, both valid:

Form 1 (average out the finer): E[E[X∣Y,Z]∣Z]=E[X∣Z] Conditioning on Y additionally inside, then averaging Y away outside, leaves Z.

Form 2 (adding coarser conditioning outside does nothing): E[E[X∣Z]∣Y,Z]=E[X∣Z] E[X∣Z] is already a function of Z. Conditioning additionally on Y outside cannot change a quantity that depends only on Z — “taking out what is known.”

Form 2 is the “taking out what is known” property in disguise. If you already have Z in the outer conditioning, anything that depends only on Z passes through unchanged.

4. Worked Examples

4.1 Three-Level Hierarchy

Θ∼prior, Λ∣Θ∼something, X∣Λ,Θ∼Poisson(Λ).

Suppose E[X∣Λ,Θ]=Λ (Poisson mean). What is E[X∣Θ]?

E[X∣Θ]=E[E[X∣Λ,Θ]∣Θ]=E[Λ∣Θ]

The tower collapses one level: X-given-(Λ,Θ) averaged over Λ becomes X-given-Θ. To go all the way:

E[X]=E[E[X∣Θ]]=E[E[Λ∣Θ]]=E[Λ]

Each application of Adam’s Law strips one layer of conditioning.

4.2 Order Statistics Conditional Mean

Let U(1)​≤⋯≤U(n)​ be order statistics of n i.i.d. Uniform(0,1). What is E[U(k)​∣U(j)​] for j<k?

Given U(j)​=u, the variables U(j+1)​,…,U(n)​ are the order statistics of n−j i.i.d. Uniforms on (u,1) (by the memoryless-like property of order statistics given an intermediate value). So:

E[U(k)​∣U(j)​=u]=u+(1−u)⋅n−j+1k−j​

The k-th order statistic among the upper n−j uniforms on (u,1) sits at position (k−j)/(n−j+1) of the remaining interval (u,1).

Now verify Adam’s Law: E[E[U(k)​∣U(j)​]]=E[U(k)​]?

E![U(j)​+(1−U(j)​)n−j+1k−j​]=n+1j​+(1−n+1j​)n−j+1k−j​

=n+1j​+n+1n−j+1​⋅n−j+1k−j​=n+1j​+n+1k−j​=n+1k​=E[U(k)​]✓

A nice sanity check: the tower collapses correctly, and the answer k/(n+1) from Order Statistics comes out.

4.3 Conditional Variance via Eve’s Law with Extra Conditioning

Eve’s Law also has an extra-conditioning version:

Var(X∣Z)=E[Var(X∣Y,Z)∣Z]+Var(E[X∣Y,Z]∣Z)

Proof. Apply Eve’s Law in the conditional universe given Z=z, then unfreeze z. ■

Reading it:

  • Var(X∣Z) = total conditional variance given Z
  • E[Var(X∣Y,Z)∣Z] = unexplained variance (noise within Y-groups, averaged over Y)
  • Var(E[X∣Y,Z]∣Z) = explained variance (spread of conditional means across Y-values)

Same decomposition as ordinary Eve’s Law, but everything lives in the Z-conditional universe.

5. The Martingale in Disguise

Plant this seed now — it will matter in Stat 212.

Define Mn​=E[X∣Y1​,…,Yn​] for a sequence Y1​,Y2​,… of observed variables. Then (Mn​) is a martingale:

E[Mn​∣M1​,…,Mn−1​]=Mn−1​

Why? Mn−1​=E[X∣Y1​,…,Yn−1​]. By the tower property:

E[Mn​∣Y1​,…,Yn−1​]=E[E[X∣Y1​,…,Yn​]∣Y1​,…,Yn−1​]=E[X∣Y1​,…,Yn−1​]=Mn−1​

The Tower Property is the martingale property. Every time you update a Bayesian posterior as new data arrives, you’re running a martingale. The sequence of posterior means is a martingale with respect to the data filtration.

6. Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Collapsing without nesting.

E[E[X∣Y]∣Z]=E[X∣Z]in general

This would require Z to be “coarser” than Y, i.e. Z=g(Y) for some g. If Z is independent of Y, the left side is E[E[X∣Y]]=E[X], not E[X∣Z] (unless X⊥Z).

Mistake 2: Forgetting the background Z in the inner expectation.

Writing E[E[X∣Y]∣Z] when you mean E[E[X∣Y,Z]∣Z] is a different object. In hierarchical models where Z affects the distribution of X directly, you must include Z in the inner conditioning.

Mistake 3: Applying tower to variance directly.

Var(X)=E[Var(X∣Y)]

This ignores the explained variance term. The correct statement is Eve’s Law. Variance does not telescope the way expectation does.

7. Summary

FormStatementWhen to use
Basic AdamE[E[X∣Y]]=E[X]No background conditioning
Tower (strip outer)E[E[X∣Y,Z]∣Z]=E[X∣Z]Average away finer conditioning Y
Tower (add coarser)E[E[X∣Z]∣Y,Z]=E[X∣Z]Known function of Z is unchanged
Eve’s with ZVar(X∣Z)=E[Var(X∣Y,Z)∣Z]+Var(E[X∣Y,Z]∣Z)Variance decomposition in Z-universe

The one-line rule: The tower collapses the inner conditioning to match the outer, provided the outer is coarser. Coarser wins.
