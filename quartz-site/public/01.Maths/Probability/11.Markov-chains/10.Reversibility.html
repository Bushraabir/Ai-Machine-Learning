Prerequisite notes: Stationary Distributions · Existence and Uniqueness of Stationary Distributions · Classification of States

“Detailed balance is the condition that says the chain looks the same run forwards and backwards. Global balance says inflow equals outflow at every state. Detailed balance says inflow equals outflow on every directed edge. The second implies the first, strictly. The gap between them is where irreversible chains live — and irreversibility turns out to be physically meaningful, not just a mathematical curiosity.”

Motivation: Two Notions of Equilibrium

From Stationary Distributions, a distribution s is stationary if sQ=s, which entrywise says:

∑i​si​qij​=sj​for all j

Rewrite this as:

∑i=j​si​qij​=sj​∑k=j​qjk​

Left side: total probability flux flowing into state j from all other states.
Right side: total probability flux flowing out of state j to all other states.

This is global balance: at stationarity, inflow = outflow at every state. It’s a conservation law — the probability mass sitting at j doesn’t change over time because what comes in equals what goes out.

Now ask a finer question: can I require balance edge by edge, not just globally per state? That is:

si​qij​=sj​qji​for all pairs i,j

This is detailed balance — and it is strictly stronger. A chain satisfying detailed balance is called reversible.

This note is about the relationship between these two conditions, why reversibility is the right framework for thinking about time-reversal, and how to use detailed balance as a computational tool.

Detailed Balance

Definition

A probability distribution s satisfies detailed balance with respect to transition matrix Q if:

si​qij​=sj​qji​for all i,j∈S

The quantity si​qij​ is the probability flux from i to j at stationarity: the probability of being in state i (which is si​) times the probability of jumping to j given you’re in i (which is qij​). Detailed balance says this flux is symmetric — whatever flows from i to j flows equally from j to i.

Theorem: Detailed Balance Implies Stationarity

If s satisfies detailed balance with respect to Q, then s is stationary: sQ=s.

Proof. Fix any state j. Sum the detailed balance condition over all i:

∑i∈S​si​qij​=∑i∈S​sj​qji​=sj​∑i∈S​qji​=sj​⋅1=sj​

The left side is (sQ)j​. So (sQ)j​=sj​ for all j, i.e., sQ=s. ■

The proof is one line. Detailed balance is the local condition; summing it over all i gives the global condition for free. This is why detailed balance is so useful as a computational tool — if I can verify the local pairwise condition, I get stationarity for free, without solving the full balance equations sQ=s as a linear system.

The Converse Is False

A chain can be stationary without satisfying detailed balance. The canonical counterexample:

Three-state cycle: S=1,2,3 with q12​=q23​=q31​=1 and all other qij​=0.

The stationary distribution is s=(1/3,1/3,1/3) (by symmetry, or solve sQ=s). Check detailed balance for the pair (1,2):

s1​q12​=31​⋅1=31​,s2​q21​=31​⋅0=0

These are not equal. So detailed balance fails — this is an irreversible chain. Probability flows around the cycle in one direction only; the time-reversed chain would flow in the opposite direction, and the two are distinguishable.

Reversibility

The Time-Reversal of a Chain

Let (Xn​)0≤n≤T​ be a Markov chain run for T steps in stationarity (i.e., X0​∼s where s is stationary). Define the time-reversed process:

Yn​=XT−n​,0≤n≤T

Claim: (Yn​) is also a Markov chain, with transition matrix:

q~​ij​=si​sj​qji​​

Proof. First, verify the Markov property for (Yn​). Given Yn​=XT−n​=i and the past Y0​,…,Yn−1​ (which is the future XT​,…,XT−n+1​), the future of Y is Yn+1​=XT−n−1​, which depends only on XT−n​=i by the Markov property of (Xn​).

Now compute the transition probability:

P(Yn+1​=j∣Yn​=i)=P(XT−n−1​=j∣XT−n​=i)

By Bayes’ theorem:

=P(XT−n​=i)P(XT−n​=i∣XT−n−1​=j)⋅P(XT−n−1​=j)​

Since the chain is in stationarity, P(Xk​=ℓ)=sℓ​ for all k,ℓ:

=si​qji​⋅sj​​=q~​ij​

■

The reversed chain has transition matrix Q~​ with entries q~​ij​=sj​qji​/si​.

Definition: Reversibility

A Markov chain (Xn​) in stationarity is reversible if the time-reversed chain has the same distribution as the forward chain. That is, for all n and all paths i0​,i1​,…,in​:

P(X0​=i0​,X1​=i1​,…,Xn​=in​)=P(X0​=in​,X1​=in−1​,…,Xn​=i0​)

In other words: you cannot tell whether the chain is running forwards or backwards by observing its distribution.

Theorem: Reversibility ⇔ Detailed Balance

A chain with stationary distribution s is reversible if and only if it satisfies detailed balance: si​qij​=sj​qji​ for all i,j.

Proof.

(⇒) Suppose the chain is reversible. Then Q~​=Q (the reversed chain has the same transitions). So q~​ij​=qij​, i.e., sj​qji​/si​=qij​, i.e., sj​qji​=si​qij​. This is detailed balance.

(⇐) Suppose detailed balance holds. Then q~​ij​=sj​qji​/si​=qij​ — the reversed chain has the same transition matrix as the forward chain. The initial distribution of (Yn​) at time 0 is Y0​=XT​∼s (stationarity). So (Yn​) and (Xn​) are both Markov chains with transition matrix Q started from s — they have the same distribution. ■

This theorem is the reason “detailed balance” and “reversibility” are used interchangeably. They’re the same condition, just stated in two different registers: detailed balance is the algebraic condition si​qij​=sj​qji​, and reversibility is the probabilistic condition that the chain is time-symmetric. Proving they’re equivalent is the content of the theorem.

Using Detailed Balance to Find Stationary Distributions

This is the main practical payoff. Instead of solving the linear system sQ=s, ∑i​si​=1 — which for large state spaces is a painful system — I can:

  1. Guess a candidate s.
  2. Check si​qij​=sj​qji​ for all pairs (i,j) with qij​>0.
  3. Normalize.

If detailed balance holds, s is stationary. This avoids solving the full system.

Why This Is Easier

The global balance equation ∑i​si​qij​=sj​ couples all states simultaneously — it’s a system of ∣S∣ equations. Detailed balance gives ∣S∣2/2 pairwise equations, each involving only two states. For chains with special structure (birth-death, graphs with nice symmetry), these pairwise equations decouple and can be solved by telescoping.

The Birth-Death Chain: Canonical Example

A birth-death chain on 0,1,…,M has transitions only to adjacent states:

qk,k+1​=pk​,qk,k−1​=rk​,qkk​=1−pk​−rk​

with pM​=r0​=0 (reflecting boundaries).

Claim: Every birth-death chain satisfies detailed balance with respect to some distribution.

Proof. Detailed balance for adjacent pair (k,k+1):

sk​qk,k+1​=sk+1​qk+1,k​

sk​pk​=sk+1​rk+1​

sk+1​=sk​⋅rk+1​pk​​

For non-adjacent pairs (i,j) with ∣i−j∣≥2: qij​=qji​=0, so si​qij​=sj​qji​=0 trivially. Detailed balance holds.

Telescoping to find s:

sk​=s0​⋅r1​r2​⋯rk​p0​p1​⋯pk−1​​

Define ρk​=r1​r2​⋯rk​p0​p1​⋯pk−1​​ (with ρ0​=1). Then sk​=s0​ρk​, and normalization gives:

s0​=∑k=0M​ρk​1​,sk​=∑ℓ=0M​ρℓ​ρk​​

Birth-death chains are always reversible. The intuition: transitions only go left or right, one step at a time. The only way to get from j to i is to pass through every state in between — and the same is true in reverse. There’s no “directionality” to exploit; every path from i to j has a mirror path from j to i. The topology forces reversibility.

Concrete Example: Ehrenfest Urn

M balls distributed between two urns, total d balls. State = number in urn 1. Transitions: pick a ball uniformly at random, move it to the other urn.

pk​=dd−k​,rk​=dk​

The ratios:

rk​pk−1​​=k/d(d−k+1)/d​=kd−k+1​

So:

ρk​=∏j=1k​jd−j+1​=(kd​)

The stationary distribution is:

sk​=∑ℓ=0d​(ℓd​)(kd​)​=2d(kd​)​

which is Binomial(d,1/2). At equilibrium, balls are distributed binomially — exactly the maximum-entropy distribution over 0,…,d with mean d/2.

This makes physical sense: the Ehrenfest urn models diffusion. At equilibrium, the distribution of particles between two sides of a container is Binomial. Reversibility is the probabilistic statement of microscopic reversibility in physics.

A Non-Example: The Three-State Cycle

Return to the three-state cycle q12​=q23​=q31​=1. I showed detailed balance fails. What is the reversed chain?

q~​ij​=si​sj​qji​​=1/3(1/3)qji​​=qji​

So Q~​=Q⊤, i.e., the reversed chain is the cycle in the opposite direction: q21​=q32​=q13​=1. The forward chain cycles 1→2→3→1→⋯ and the reversed chain cycles 1→3→2→1→⋯. These are distinguishable — the chain is not reversible.

This is the archetypal irreversible chain. In physics, irreversibility corresponds to entropy production — systems that run their dynamics in a direction determined by the arrow of time. The three-state cycle is the simplest example.

Kolmogorov’s Criterion

For a general (not necessarily birth-death) chain, there is a clean necessary and sufficient condition for reversibility that doesn’t require knowing s in advance.

Theorem (Kolmogorov’s Cycle Criterion)

An irreducible Markov chain is reversible (with respect to its unique stationary distribution) if and only if for every cycle i0​→i1​→⋯→in​→i0​ in the state space:

qi0​i1​​qi1​i2​​⋯qin−1​in​​qin​i0​​=qi0​in​​qin​in−1​​⋯qi1​i0​​

The probability of traversing the cycle in one direction equals the probability of traversing it in the opposite direction.

Proof sketch (⇒): If detailed balance holds, multiply the detailed balance equations around the cycle:

∏k=0n−1​sik​​qik​ik+1​​=∏k=0n−1​sik+1​​qik+1​ik​​

The left product is ∏k​sik​​⋅∏k​qik​ik+1​​, and similarly for the right. The s factors are the same on both sides (since the sik​​ appear once each on each side around the cycle). Cancel them to get the cycle condition. ■

Why this matters: Kolmogorov’s criterion is checkable from Q alone, without knowing s. For complicated state spaces, checking all cycles is hard — but for small graphs it’s immediate.

For the three-state cycle: the only non-trivial cycle is 1→2→3→1 (and its reverse). Forward product: q12​q23​q31​=1⋅1⋅1=1. Reverse product: q13​q32​q21​=0⋅0⋅0=0. Not equal. Not reversible. ✓

The Reversed Chain Has the Same Stationary Distribution

Claim: The reversed chain Q~​ has the same stationary distribution s as the forward chain Q.

Proof. Check sQ~​=s:

(sQ~​)j​=∑i​si​q~​ij​=∑i​si​si​sj​qji​​=sj​∑i​qji​=sj​⋅1=sj​

■

So reversibility is a property of a chain relative to its own stationary distribution. The forward and reversed chains share the same s, even when Q=Q~​ (i.e., even when the chain is irreversible).

Reversibility in MCMC: Why It Matters Computationally

The most important application of reversibility in modern probability is Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC). The goal: sample from a target distribution π that I can evaluate (up to a normalizing constant) but cannot sample from directly.

The strategy: Construct a Markov chain whose stationary distribution is π, run it until it mixes, and treat the samples as approximate draws from π.

The problem: How do I construct such a chain? The detailed balance condition gives a sufficient condition that’s easy to engineer:

πi​qij​=πj​qji​for all i,j

If I design Q to satisfy detailed balance with respect to π, then π is automatically stationary. This is how the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm works.

Metropolis-Hastings at a Glance

Given target π and a proposal kernel r(i,j) (symmetric for simplicity: r(i,j)=r(j,i)):

  1. At state i, propose moving to j with probability r(i,j).
  2. Accept the proposal with probability min(1,πi​πj​​).
  3. If rejected, stay at i.

The resulting transition probabilities:

qij​=r(i,j)⋅min!(1,πi​πj​​)(i=j)

Verify detailed balance:

πi​qij​=πi​⋅r(i,j)⋅min!(1,πi​πj​​)

Case πj​≤πi​: min(1,πj​/πi​)=πj​/πi​, so πi​qij​=r(i,j)πj​.
And: πj​qji​=πj​⋅r(j,i)⋅min(1,πi​/πj​)=πj​⋅r(i,j)⋅1=πj​r(i,j). Equal. ✓

Case πj​>πi​: symmetric argument. ✓

So Metropolis-Hastings is designed to satisfy detailed balance with respect to π — by construction. I never need to know the normalizing constant of π (it cancels in the ratio πj​/πi​). The algorithm samples from distributions I can only evaluate up to a constant. This is the miracle of MCMC, and detailed balance is the mathematical mechanism behind it.

This is the reason physicists care about detailed balance. In statistical mechanics, the Boltzmann distribution πi​∝e−Ei​/kT (where Ei​ is the energy of state i) is the equilibrium distribution of a physical system at temperature T. To sample from it computationally, you run Metropolis-Hastings, which satisfies detailed balance with respect to π — the same condition that holds for physical systems in thermal equilibrium. The algorithm is simulating thermodynamic equilibrium.

One-Line Summary for Future Me

Detailed balance si​qij​=sj​qji​ is the pairwise version of stationarity — it implies sQ=s immediately by summing over i. A chain satisfying it is reversible: the time-reversed chain q~​ij​=sj​qji​/si​ equals the forward chain. Birth-death chains are always reversible (telescope the pairwise equations). Kolmogorov’s criterion detects reversibility from Q alone by checking whether cycle products are direction-symmetric. MCMC (Metropolis-Hastings) is engineered to satisfy detailed balance with respect to the target distribution π — that’s the entire design principle.
