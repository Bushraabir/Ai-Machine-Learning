04.Thinning of Poisson Processes

04.Thinning of Poisson Processes

Apr 02, 20269 min read

Type: Theorem | Domain: Stochastic Processes, Probability
Prerequisite map: Poisson Process 1D → Bernoulli Trials → Independence → Thinning
Parent note: Poisson Process 1D
Sibling notes: Superposition | Conditioning
Relevant courses: Harvard Stat 110 (Lecture 14), MIT 18.650

1. The Setup

You have a Poisson process PP(λ). Each arriving event is independently kept with probability p, or discarded with probability 1−p.

This random filtering is called thinning (also: independent marking, Bernoulli thinning).

Original PP(λ):
────┼────┼──┼─────┼──┼────┼───┼──▶
    e₁   e₂  e₃    e₄  e₅   e₆   e₇

Each event: keep (↓) w.p. p, discard (✗) w.p. 1-p

    ✓    ✗   ✓     ✗   ✓    ✓    ✗

Kept stream PP(λp):
────┼───────┼──────────┼────┼──────▶
    e₁      e₃         e₅   e₆

Discarded stream PP(λ(1-p)):
─────────┼───────┼──────────────┼──▶
         e₂      e₄             e₇

2. The Theorem

Theorem (Thinning / Decomposition):

Let N∼PP(λ). Independently mark each event as type 1 with probability p and type 2 with probability 1−p. Define:

  • N1​(t) = number of type-1 events in [0,t]
  • N2​(t) = number of type-2 events in [0,t]

Then:

N1​(t)∼PP(λp)andN2​(t)∼PP(λ(1−p))​

N1​⊥N2​​

The two thinned streams are independent Poisson processes.

3. The Surprise — Independence

The rates λp and λ(1−p) are expected. The independence is the deep result.

Intuitively: knowing that many events were kept (large N1​) tells you nothing about how many were discarded (N2​), because each decision was made independently coin flip by coin flip.

But this intuition is incomplete — N1​ and N2​ are both functions of the same underlying process N. Why doesn’t knowing N1​ tell you something about N=N1​+N2​ and hence about N2​?

The answer lies in the Poisson distribution’s special property:

Key algebraic fact: If N∼Poisson(μ) and N1​∣N∼Binomial(N,p), then N1​∼Poisson(μp) and N1​⊥N2​=N−N1​.

This is unique to the Poisson distribution — no other distribution has this property.

4. Proof

Proof of Marginal Distributions

Fix time interval [0,t]. Let N=N(t)∼Poisson(λt).

Each event is independently type 1 with probability p, so:

N1​∣N∼Binomial(N,p)

Compute P(N1​=k) by conditioning on N:

P(N1​=k)=∑n=k∞​P(N1​=k∣N=n)⋅P(N=n)

=∑n=k∞​(kn​)pk(1−p)n−k⋅n!e−λt(λt)n​

=k!e−λtpk(λt)k​∑n=k∞​(n−k)!(λt)n−k(1−p)n−k​

Let m=n−k:

=k!e−λt(pλt)k​∑m=0∞​m!(λt(1−p))m​

=k!e−λt(pλt)k​⋅eλt(1−p)

=k!e−λtp(pλt)k​

⟹N1​∼Poisson(λtp)​✓

By symmetry: N2​∼Poisson(λt(1−p)).

Proof of Independence

Compute the joint PMF of (N1​,N2​):

P(N1​=j,,N2​=k)=P(N1​=j,,N=j+k)

=P(N1​=j∣N=j+k)⋅P(N=j+k)

=(jj+k​)pj(1−p)k⋅(j+k)!e−λt(λt)j+k​

=j!,k!(j+k)!​pj(1−p)k⋅(j+k)!e−λt(λt)j+k​

=j!e−λtp(λtp)j​⋅k!e−λt(1−p)(λt(1−p))k​

=P(N1​=j)⋅P(N2​=k)✓

The joint PMF factors — N1​ and N2​ are independent. ✓

5. Generalization — k-Type Thinning

Each event is independently assigned to type i with probability pi​, where ∑i=1k​pi​=1.

N1​∼PP(λp1​),;N2​∼PP(λp2​),;…,;Nk​∼PP(λpk​)​

N1​,N2​,…,Nk​ mutually independent​

Proof: Same as above using Multinomial instead of Binomial. The joint PMF factors into a product of Poisson PMFs.

Example: Customers at a store choose product A (40%), B (35%), C (25%). If customers arrive as PP(100/hour):

  • Product A purchases: PP(40/hour)
  • Product B purchases: PP(35/hour)
  • Product C purchases: PP(25/hour)
  • All three streams are mutually independent

6. Non-Constant Thinning — State-Dependent Marking

Thinning can be generalized: the retention probability p(t) varies with time.

Each event at time t is kept with probability p(t)

Result: The thinned process is still Poisson but with a time-varying rate:

N1​∼PP(λ(t)⋅p(t))(inhomogeneous PP)

The rate at time t becomes λ⋅p(t).

Example: A server processes requests at rate 100/sec. During peak hours (8am–6pm), each request has a p(t)=0.8 chance of being a “complex” request. Complex requests form an inhomogeneous PP with rate 80/sec during peak hours.

7. Duality with Superposition

Thinning and superposition are exact inverses:

                    SUPERPOSITION
         PP(λ₁) ──┐
                   ├──→  PP(λ₁ + λ₂)
         PP(λ₂) ──┘

                      THINNING
                              ┌──→  PP(λp)       [kept]
         PP(λ)  ──────────────┤
                              └──→  PP(λ(1-p))   [discarded]

The full picture:

SuperpositionThinning
Directionn→1 (merge)1→n (split)
Rateλ1​+λ2​λp, λ(1−p)
IndependenceInput streams must be independentOutput streams become independent
Key toolPGF factoringPoisson-Binomial-Poisson identity

Round-trip consistency:

Thin PP(λ) with prob p → get PP(λp) and PP(λ(1−p)).
Now superpose them → PP(λp+λ(1−p)) = PP(λ). ✓

You recover the original rate exactly.

8. The Poisson-Binomial-Poisson Identity

This is the algebraic engine behind thinning. Worth stating cleanly:

Theorem: If N∼Poisson(μ) and X∣N∼Binomial(N,p), then:

X∼Poisson(μp)andX⊥(N−X)

This is unique to Poisson. Check: if N∼Binomial(m,q) and X∣N∼Binomial(N,p), then X∼Binomial(m,pq) but X and N−X are not independent.

The Poisson distribution is the only distribution closed under Binomial thinning with output independence.

9. Connection to the Conditioning Theorem

Thinning and conditioning are related through a beautiful symmetry.

Conditioning theorem says: Given N(T)=n, events are uniform on [0,T].

Thinning says: Thin PP(λ) by keeping each event with probability p=s/T (i.e., keep if event falls in [0,s]).

Then:

  • Kept stream = PP(λ⋅s/T) on [0,T], which equals PP(λ) on [0,s]
  • This is exactly N(s)

So thinning by a spatial indicator (1[event∈[0,s]]) recovers the sub-interval count, and the independence of the thinned streams recovers the independent increments property.

Deep link: The independence of increments of a Poisson process is thinning independence, when the thinning is by time sub-interval membership.

10. Worked Examples

Example 1 — Network Packets

Packets arrive at a router as PP(λ=1000/sec).

  • 60% are TCP packets
  • 30% are UDP packets
  • 10% are ICMP packets

TCP stream: PP(600/sec)
UDP stream: PP(300/sec)
ICMP stream: PP(100/sec)
All mutually independent.

P(no ICMP in 10ms)=e−100×0.01=e−1≈0.368

P(exactly 5 TCP and exactly 2 UDP in 10ms):

=P(NTCP​=5)⋅P(NUDP​=2)(by independence)

=5!e−6⋅65​⋅2!e−3⋅32​≈0.1606×0.2240≈0.036

Example 2 — Quality Control

Items produced as PP(λ=50/hour). Each is defective with probability p=0.04.

  • Defective items: PP(2/hour)
  • Good items: PP(48/hour)
  • Independent streams

E[time until first defective]=1/2 hour = 30 minutes

P(0 defectives in 2 hours)=e−2×2=e−4≈0.018

Example 3 — Thinning Then Race

Cars arrive on a highway as PP(λ=60/min). Each is independently a truck w.p. 0.1 or car w.p. 0.9.

Truck stream: PP(6/min), Car stream: PP(54/min).

P(first vehicle is a truck)=606​=0.1 ← same as thinning probability. Always.

General rule: P(first thinned event is type i)=pi​. The race outcome probability equals the thinning probability.

11. Common Mistakes

MistakeCorrection
Assuming independence is obviousN1​ and N2​ share the same parent N — independence is a theorem, not automatic
Applying thinning without independence of marksMarks must be independent of each other and of the process history
Confusing thinning probability with conditional probabilityp is the probability of keeping an event, not the probability given some condition
Forgetting thinned streams are full Poisson processesNot just the right mean — full independent increments, stationary, etc.

13. Key Formulas Cheatsheet

N∼PP(λ),;p-thin;⇒;N1​∼PP(λp)⊥N2​∼PP(λ(1−p))​

k-type thinning: Ni​∼PP(λpi​),N1​⊥N2​⊥⋯⊥Nk​​

14. Mental Model

Imagine a factory line (the Poisson process) producing widgets at rate λ. At the end of the line, a quality inspector independently flips a coin for each widget: heads → keep it, tails → scrap it.

The kept widgets form their own independent assembly line at rate λp. The scrapped widgets form their own independent scrap heap at rate λ(1−p). The two piles have no memory of each other — because each decision was made independently.

The magic: the scrapping process has no knowledge of how many were kept, and vice versa. The independence emerges from the independence of the coin flips and the Poisson structure of the original process.
