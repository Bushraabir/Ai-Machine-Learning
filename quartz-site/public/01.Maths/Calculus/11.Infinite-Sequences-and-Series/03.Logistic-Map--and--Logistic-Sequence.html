“Boundless complexity from utter simplicity.”

A single quadratic recurrence. One parameter. And hidden inside it: the entire zoo of dynamical behaviours — fixed points, period-doubling, chaos, universality. This is one of the most important equations in all of applied mathematics.

The Equation

xn+1​=r,xn​(1−xn​),x0​∈(0,1),r∈(0,4]​

That is the entire object of study. One quadratic map on [0,1]. The parameter r controls the behaviour, and the range of behaviour as r varies is extraordinary.

Personal note: Pierre François Verhulst introduced the logistic differential equation in 1838 as a model of population growth with a carrying capacity. The discrete analogue — the logistic map — was popularised by Robert May in his landmark 1976 Nature paper, which introduced it to biologists and arguably birthed the modern study of chaos. Read that paper. It is three pages and changed everything.

Motivation: Discrete Population Growth

The Malthusian (Unconstrained) Model

Start simple. If a population xn​ grows by a factor r each generation:

xn+1​=rxn​

Solution: xn​=rnx0​. Exponential growth — unbounded. Unrealistic.

The Logistic Correction

Introduce a carrying capacity. Normalize so the maximum population is 1. The growth rate is penalized as xn​→1:

xn+1​=rxn​penalty term(1−xn​)​​

  • When xn​≈0: nearly unconstrained, xn+1​≈rxn​.
  • When xn​≈1: population at carrying capacity, xn+1​≈0.
  • The factor (1−xn​) is the fraction of unused capacity.

This is the logistic map. The full behaviour as r varies from 0 to 4 spans fixed points, cycles, and chaos.

Fixed Points & Stability — The Full Analysis

Finding Fixed Points

A fixed point x^_ satisfies f(x^_) = x^* where f(x)=rx(1−x):

rx^_(1 - x^_) = x^*

x^_[r(1-x^_) - 1] = 0

Two fixed points:

x∗=0​andx∗=rr−1​​(r>1)

The second fixed point only lies in (0,1) when r>1.

Stability Analysis

A fixed point x^_ is stable (attracting) if |f'(x^_)| < 1, and unstable (repelling) if ∣f′(x∗)∣>1.

Compute the derivative:

f′(x)=r(1−2x)

__At x^_ = 0:_*

f′(0)=r

  • Stable when r<1.
  • Unstable when r>1.

__At x^_ = (r-1)/r:_*

f′!(rr−1​)=r(1−r2(r−1)​)=2−r

  • Stable when ∣2−r∣<1, i.e., 1<r<3.
  • At r=3: ∣f′(x∗)∣=1 — bifurcation point.
  • Unstable when r>3.

Bifurcations and the Period-Doubling Cascade

What is a Bifurcation?

At r=3, the fixed point x∗=(r−1)/r loses stability — it transitions from attracting to repelling. Simultaneously, a 2-cycle is born: two points xa​,xb​ with f(xa​)=xb​ and f(xb​)=xa​.

Finding the 2-Cycle

The 2-cycle points satisfy f(f(x))=x but f(x)=x. Factoring out the fixed points from f2(x)−x=0, the 2-cycle points are:

xa,b​=2r(r+1)±(r+1)(r−3)​​

These are real only when r≥3 — the 2-cycle is born exactly at the bifurcation r=3.

Stability of the 2-Cycle

By the chain rule: dxd​f2(x)​x=xa​​=f′(xb​)⋅f′(xa​)=(2−r)2+(correction)

Working through the algebra: the 2-cycle is stable for 3<r<1+6​≈3.4495.

Chaos: Sensitive Dependence on Initial Conditions

Definition (Devaney)

The logistic map at r>r∞​ is chaotic in the sense of:

  1. Sensitive dependence: ∃,δ>0 such that for any x0​ and any ε>0, there exists y0​ with ∣x0​−y0​∣<ε yet ∣xn​−yn​∣>δ for some n.
  2. Topological transitivity: There exists an orbit dense in [0,1].
  3. Dense periodic orbits: Periodic points are dense.

Conjugacy at r=4

At r=4 the logistic map is topologically conjugate to the tent/doubling map via xn​=sin2(πθn​):

θn+1​=2θn​(mod1)

This is the doubling map: it shifts binary digits of θ0​. Any uncertainty in the last bit of θ0​ propagates to the first bit after n steps. The Lyapunov exponent at r=4 is exactly λ=ln2.

Feigenbaum’s Universality Constant

In 1975, Mitchell Feigenbaum computed ratios of successive bifurcation intervals:

δ=limk→∞​rk+1​−rk​rk​−rk−1​​

This constant is universal: it appears in the period-doubling cascade of any unimodal map with a smooth quadratic maximum — sine map, Hénon map restricted to 1D, and many others. The specific equation doesn’t matter; only the local shape of the maximum matters.

Feigenbaum proved this using renormalization group ideas borrowed from quantum field theory — the same mathematical structure that governs phase transitions in statistical mechanics.

The Feigenbaum constant is transcendental and has no known closed form in terms of π, e, or other classical constants. It is a genuinely new mathematical constant.

Interpretation:

  • λ<0: attracting periodic orbit

  • λ>0: chaos — exponential separation of nearby orbits

The Connection to the Logistic Function (Sigmoid)

The logistic function (sigmoid):

σ(t)=1+e−t1​

is the solution to the continuous logistic ODE dp/dt=rp(1−p):

p(t)=σ!(rt+ln1−p0​p0​​)

The logistic map is the Euler discretization of this ODE with step size 1:

pn+1​≈pn​+rpn​(1−pn​)

which is a logistic map with parameter 1+r. The sigmoid is the equilibrium of the continuous flow; the logistic map iterates the discrete approximation and discovers the rich bifurcation structure that the smooth ODE solution conceals.

Key algebraic link. The derivative of σ:

σ′(z)=σ(z)(1−σ(z))

is exactly the logistic map expression x(1−x) evaluated at x=σ(z). This is not a coincidence — it is why the logistic function is the canonical activation for binary classification, and why backpropagation through sigmoid layers involves the logistic map’s own derivative structure.

Connection to AI / ML / Deep Learning

1. The Sigmoid Neuron and the Vanishing Gradient Problem

The activation σ(z)=1/(1+e−z) maps R→(0,1). Its gradient σ′(z)=σ(z)(1−σ(z))≤1/4 — saturating to 0 when ∣z∣ is large.

In a deep network of L sigmoid layers, backpropagation multiplies gradients:

∂w1​∂L​=∂xL​∂L​⋅∏k=1L​σ′(zk​)⋅Wk​

Each σ′(zk​)≤1/4, so ∏k=1L​σ′(zk​)≤(1/4)L→0 exponentially. This is the vanishing gradient problem — directly caused by the logistic map’s derivative being bounded away from 1 at its fixed points. ReLU (f′(z)=1 in the active region) was introduced precisely to escape this.

2. Bifurcations in RNNs — Exploding and Vanishing Gradients

A vanilla RNN updates:

hn+1​=tanh(Whn​+Uxn​+b)

The Jacobian of this map is Jn​=W⋅diag(1−hn2​). Backpropagating through time multiplies these Jacobians:

∂h0​∂hT​​=∏n=0T−1​Jn​

If ρ(W)>1: eigenvalues of the product grow — exploding gradients (chaos regime of logistic map). If ρ(W)<1: eigenvalues shrink — vanishing gradients (stable fixed point regime).

The LSTM’s gating mechanism is a learned bifurcation controller: the forget gate ft​=σ(Wf​ht−1​+Uf​xt​) adaptively scales the recurrent eigenvalues to stay near 1 — precisely the r≈3 boundary between stability and chaos.

3. Echo State Networks — Edge of Chaos

Reservoir Computing (Echo State Networks) designs a fixed random RNN and trains only the readout. The design principle is the edge of chaos: spectral radius ρ(W)≈1.

This is exactly the logistic map at r≈3:

  • ρ<1 (stable): reservoir forgets inputs, poor memory
  • ρ>1 (chaotic): sensitive dependence, unreliable computation
  • ρ≈1 (edge): maximum memory capacity and computational richness

This is a theorem in reservoir computing (Jaeger 2001), and it is Feigenbaum’s story rewritten in N×N matrix language.

4. Neural ODEs — Discretization Chaos

A Neural ODE learns dz/dt=fθ​(z,t) and integrates it numerically. The Euler method with step h:

zn+1​=zn​+hfθ​(zn​)

applied to the logistic ODE dp/dt=p(1−p) gives:

pn+1​=pn​(1+h−hpn​)

a logistic map with effective r=1+h. For h small: stable fixed point (correct). For h large (say h=2.5): r=3.5 — period-4 oscillations from a smooth ODE whose exact solution has no oscillation at all.

Lesson: naive large-step discretization of physics can produce spurious chaos. This motivates adaptive solvers (Dormand-Prince, Runge-Kutta 4/5) inside Neural ODE training, where step size is controlled to keep the effective r safely below the bifurcation threshold.

5. SGD at the Edge of Stability

For learning rate η on a loss with sharpness λmax​(∇2L), the linearized gradient descent update is:

wn+1​=wn​−η∇L(wn​)

Stability requires ηλmax​<2 — directly analogous to requiring r<3 in the logistic map. Recent work (Cohen et al., ICLR 2021) shows that Adam and SGD with large η operate in the Edge of Stability regime where ηλmax​≈2 throughout training — the network self-organises to the bifurcation boundary, similar to how some dynamical systems are attracted to the edge of chaos.

6. The Logistic Loss — Why It Cancels the Sigmoid Derivative

For binary classification with sigmoid output y^​=σ(z), the squared error loss is:

Lsq​=21​(σ(z)−y)2⟹∂z∂Lsq​​=(σ(z)−y)⋅σ′(z)

When σ(z)≈0 or 1 (confident but wrong), σ′(z)≈0 — the gradient vanishes. The cross-entropy (logistic) loss:

LCE​=−ylogσ(z)−(1−y)log(1−σ(z))⟹∂z∂LCE​​=σ(z)−y

The sigmoid derivative cancels because ∂(−logσ)/∂z=1−σ=(1−σ), and combined they give σ−y directly. The logistic loss is the unique loss that cancels the saturation of the logistic activation — a deep compatibility between the loss and activation rooted in the exponential family structure of the Bernoulli distribution.

7. Chaos and Generalization — Lyapunov Exponents of Training

Recent theory links generalization to the Lyapunov exponent of the SGD trajectory in parameter space. Models that generalise well tend to reside near broad, flat minima (negative Lyapunov exponent — attracting fixed points of the loss landscape dynamics). Models that overfit tend to converge to sharp, narrow minima (borderline unstable, analogous to r≈3). This is an active research area (2022–present) directly inspired by dynamical systems theory of the logistic map.

Practice Problems

  1. Show x∗=(r−1)/r is stable for 1<r<3 by showing ∣f′(x∗)∣=∣2−r∣<1 directly.
  1. For r=2, make the substitution xn​=21​(1−yn​) and find the recurrence for yn​. Solve it in closed form.
  1. Verify that the 2-cycle points (xa​,xb​) found in Example 2 satisfy f(xa​)=xb​ and f(xb​)=xa​ for r=3.2.
  1. Prove that at r=4, the substitution xn​=sin2(πθn​) transforms the logistic map into the doubling map θn+1​=2θn​(mod1).
  1. Using r1​=3, r2​=3.4495, r3​=3.5441, r4​=3.5644, compute δ1​, δ2​, δ3​ and compare to δ=4.669.
  1. (ML connection) Show that the logistic loss L=−ylogσ(z)−(1−y)log(1−σ(z)) has gradient ∂L/∂z=σ(z)−y. Explain why this is superior to squared error for sigmoid-activated neurons from a gradient-flow perspective.
  1. (Hard) Prove that the period-3 window implies chaos: if f has a period-3 orbit, then by Sharkovskii’s Theorem, it has orbits of all periods. What does this imply about the logistic map for r in the period-3 window near r≈3.83?
