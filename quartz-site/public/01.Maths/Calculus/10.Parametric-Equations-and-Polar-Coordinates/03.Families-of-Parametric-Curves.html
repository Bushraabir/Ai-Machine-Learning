“A single curve is a fact. A family of curves is a theory.” — Personal margin note, Stewart §10.1

See also: Linear Equations · Predator–Prey System · Parametric Curves

0. Preliminary Remarks

A family of curves arises when a parametric curve depends not only on a parameter t (which traces the curve) but also on one or more constants — call them c, a, k, n, etc. — whose different values generate an entire collection of related curves.

The distinction is crucial and worth stating precisely from the start:

SymbolRoleWhat changes when it varies
tTracing parameterWhich point on a fixed curve
c,a,k,…Family parameterWhich curve in the family

Families appear everywhere: in physics (orbits with different energies), in geometry (spirals with different growth rates), in engineering (Lissajous figures with different frequency ratios), and in machine learning (curves traced by training dynamics under different hyperparameters).

The deep mathematical question is: what geometric property is shared across a family, and what changes? Answering this requires studying how the curve morphs as the family parameter varies — which is calculus in disguise.

1. Formal Definition

Definition 1.1 (Family of Parametric Curves). A one-parameter family of curves is a map F:I×J→R2,(t,c)↦(x(t,c),,y(t,c)), where I is the tracing interval (in t), J is the family interval (in c), and x,y are continuous functions of both variables. For each fixed c∈J, the curve γc​:I→R2,t↦(x(t,c),y(t,c)) is called a member of the family.

A two-parameter family allows both c1​ and c2​ to vary, generating a two-dimensional collection of curves. In general, an n-parameter family fills an n-dimensional space of curves.

Remark. In the Predator–Prey System, the level sets of the conserved quantity H(x,y)=k form a one-parameter family of closed orbits, indexed by k>H(E∗). This is the standard way families arise from conservation laws.

Key observation. The number of horizontal tangencies is ω and the number of vertical tangencies is ω−1 (for δ=0, integer ω). This is how engineers read ω from an oscilloscope: count the tangencies.

When does the curve close? The curve (cos(ωt+δ),sint) is periodic iff ω is rational. If ω=p/q in lowest terms, the curve closes after t=2πq and has p vertical loops and q horizontal loops.

Proof. The curve returns to its initial position when t↦t+T satisfies sint=sin(t+T) and cos(ωt)=cos(ω(t+T)). The first requires T=2πk for integer k; the second requires ωT=2πm for integer m. So ω=m/k∈Q. The minimal period is T=2πq where ω=p/q in lowest terms. ■

Irrational ω: The curve never closes and is dense in the rectangle [−1,1]2 — it fills the rectangle in the limit (Kronecker’s theorem / equidistribution).

As δ increases from 0 to π/2, the figure-8 rotates continuously from vertical to horizontal. The family γδ​ is a continuous deformation — a homotopy — of curves.

Definition 4.1 (Homotopy of Curves). Two curves γ0​ and γ1​ in R2 are homotopic if there exists a continuous family γc​c∈[0,1]​ connecting them — i.e., a continuous map F:[a,b]×[0,1]→R2 with F(⋅,0)=γ0​ and F(⋅,1)=γ1​.

Families of parametric curves are homotopies. Phase-shifting deformations are among the most natural examples.

Recall from Parametric Curves §5.1 the cycloid is generated by a circle rolling on a line. Generalizing the geometry gives a rich family.

Definition 6.1 (Bifurcation). A value c=c0​ of the family parameter is a bifurcation point if the topological type of γc​ changes at c0​.

The transition h=r (cusp appearance) and (implicitly) the appearance of loops for h>r are both bifurcations.

Why does the topology change? At e=1, the denominator 1+cosθ vanishes at θ=π — the curve escapes to infinity. The bifurcation is the appearance of a pole in the rational parametrization.

8. Orthogonal Families — The Calculus Connection

Two families γc​ and γ~​k​ are orthogonal if at every intersection point, the tangent vectors of the two families are perpendicular.

Definition 8.1. Families F(x,y)=c and G(x,y)=k are orthogonal trajectories if ∇F⋅∇G=0 at every intersection.

How to find orthogonal trajectories of F(x,y)=c:

  1. Differentiate F(x,y)=c implicitly: find slope dy/dx=m(x,y) of the family.
  2. The orthogonal family has slope dy/dx=−1/m(x,y).
  3. Solve this ODE.

Example. Family: y=cx2 (parabolas through the origin).

Step 1: dy/dx=2cx=2(y/x2)⋅x=2y/x.

Step 2: Orthogonal family satisfies dy/dx=−x/(2y).

Step 3: 2y,dy=−x,dx⇒y2=−x2/2+k⇒x2/2+y2=k — ellipses with semi-axes ratio 2​:1.

Applications of orthogonal trajectories:

  • Electric field lines ⊥ equipotential curves (Poisson equation)
  • Streamlines ⊥ equipotential lines in fluid flow
  • Heat flow lines ⊥ isothermal curves

The orthogonal family to the level curves of the Lotka–Volterra conserved quantity H from Predator–Prey System would give curves that cut through orbits — these would be the “steepest descent” directions on the H landscape.

9. Envelope of a Family

Definition 9.1 (Envelope). The envelope of a one-parameter family F(x,y,c)=0 is a curve that is tangent to every member of the family.

The envelope is found by eliminating c from the system: F(x,y,c)=0and∂c∂F​(x,y,c)=0.

Derivation. The envelope touches γc​ at the point where γc+ε​ and γc​ intersect as ε→0. Formally: the intersection of F=0 and F+ε∂F/∂c+O(ε2)=0 gives ∂F/∂c=0 in the limit. ■

9.1 Example — Family of Lines

Family: y=cx−c2, i.e., F(x,y,c)=cx−c2−y=0.

∂F/∂c=x−2c=0⇒c=x/2.

Substitute: y=(x/2)⋅x−(x/2)2=x2/2−x2/4=x2/4.

Envelope: y=x2/4 — a parabola tangent to every line in the family.

9.2 Example — Family of Circles (Caustics)

Family: circles of radius r centered on the x-axis: (x−c)2+y2=r2.

∂F/∂c=−2(x−c)=0⇒x=c.

Substitute: y2=r2⇒y=±r.

Envelope: the two horizontal lines y=±r — the common external tangent lines.

9.3 Discriminant Interpretation

The system F=0, ∂F/∂c=0 is the condition for c to be a repeated root of F(x,y,c)=0 (viewed as a polynomial in c). The envelope is the discriminant locus — a classical algebro-geometric object.

Varying the coefficients inside gives a family of butterfly-like curves — an example of a family where the topology is constant (always a butterfly-like figure) but the metric shape deforms continuously.

12. Connection to AI / Machine Learning / Deep Learning

12.1 Hyperparameter Families — Training Curves as Parametric Families

When training a neural network with learning rate η, the loss trajectory t↦L(θ(t;η)) is a parametric curve in loss space indexed by η. Varying η gives a family of training curves:

The envelope of this family (Definition 9.1) is the curve of best achievable loss at each epoch — the Pareto frontier of training efficiency. Finding it is the problem of learning rate scheduling.

12.3 Activation Function Families

Many neural network activation functions form parametric families:

Parametric ReLU (PReLU): σ(x,α)={x​x≥0 αx​x<0​,α∈[0,1].

As α varies:

  • α=0: ReLU
  • α=1: linear (identity)
  • α=0.01: Leaky ReLU

The family σ(⋅,α)α∈[0,1]​ is a one-parameter family of piecewise-linear functions. The input-output curve x↦σ(x,α) is a parametric curve in (x,σ)-space indexed by α.

ELU family: σ(x,α)={x​x≥0 α(ex−1)​x<0​.

The graph of σ(⋅,α) is a smooth curve for each α; the family morphs the negative half continuously.

12.4 Diffusion Model Score Curves

In a diffusion model (DDPM), data x0​ is gradually noised: xt​=αˉt​​,x0​+1−αˉt​​,ϵ,t∈[0,T].

For fixed x0​, the path t↦xt​ is a parametric curve in data space — a straight-line interpolation in distribution space between data and noise. The family of such paths (over all x0​ in the training set) is a family of curves in Rd.

The score function ∇x​logpt​(x) is the envelope of this family in a precise sense: it is the direction field that is tangent to the denoising paths. Learning the score = learning the envelope.

12.5 Neural Architecture as Curve Families

A neural network with residual connections computes: hl+1​=hl​+fθ​(hl​),l=0,1,…,L−1.

In the continuous limit (Neural ODE from Parametric Curves §8.4): dtdh​=fθ​(h(t),t).

For each input x, the hidden state traces a different curve t↦h(t;x). The family of all such curves (over all inputs x) is a family of curves in feature space. The network learns to warp this family so that curves corresponding to the same class cluster together at t=1.

12.6 GANs and the Envelope of Generated Distributions

The generator Gθ​:Z→X of a GAN traces a curve in data space as z varies along a path in latent space. The envelope of all such curves (as θ varies over the training trajectory) approximates the support of the real data distribution.

Training convergence = the family of generator curves’ envelope matching the data manifold.

14. Things to Prove Yourself

  1. Prove that the logarithmic spiral r=ebt is self-similar: any rotation corresponds to a scaling. That is, show γb​(t+t0​)=ebt0​γb​(t) for all t,t0​.

  2. Derive the envelope of the family of lines xcosc+ysinc=1 (lines at distance 1 from the origin at angle c). What curve do you get?

  3. Rose curves. For r=cos(nθ) with n=p/q rational in lowest terms, prove the curve closes after θ∈[0,qπ] (if pq is odd) or [0,2qπ] (if pq is even).

  4. Prove that the orthogonal trajectories of the family of circles x2+y2=c2 are the family of lines y=kx through the origin.

  5. For the limaçon family r=1+ccosθ: find the value of c at which the inner loop first appears (bifurcation point) by finding when the curve becomes self-intersecting. (Hint: find when r=0 has a solution.)

  6. Superellipse arc length. Compute L(p)=∫02π​x′(θ)2+y′(θ)2​,dθ for the superellipse ∣x∣p+∣y∣p=1 with x=cos2/pθ, y=sin2/pθ. Verify: L(2)=2π (circle) and L(1)=42​ (perimeter of the diamond ∣x∣+∣y∣=1).

  7. Envelope and discriminant. Show that the envelope of the family y=cx−c2 (§9.1) corresponds to the discriminant of c2−cx+y=0 (viewed as a quadratic in c) being zero. Generalize: the envelope of F(x,y,c)=0 is the discriminant locus of F with respect to c.
