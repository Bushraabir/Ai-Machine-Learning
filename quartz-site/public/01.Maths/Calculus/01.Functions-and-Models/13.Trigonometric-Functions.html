1. The Unit Circle — The Right Way to Think About It

Most calculus courses introduce trig via right triangles. That’s pedagogically convenient but conceptually limiting. The correct foundational picture is the unit circle.

Definition (Unit Circle). The unit circle S1⊂R2 is the set S1=(x,y)∈R2∣x2+y2=1.

Fix a real number θ (measured in radians). Consider the point P(θ) obtained by starting at (1,0) and traversing an arc of signed length θ counterclockwise along S1.

cosθ:=x-coordinate of P(θ),sinθ:=y-coordinate of P(θ).

Why radians? One radian is the angle subtended at the center of a circle by an arc whose length equals the radius. With this convention, arc length =rθ, and the derivative formulas take their simplest form. If you use degrees, you drag a factor of π/180 everywhere — ugly and unnatural.

Personal note: Stewart §1.4 uses exactly this picture. MIT 18.02 formalizes it further once we parametrize S1 as r(t)=(cost,sint), giving velocity r′(t)=(−sint,cost) — a vector always tangent to the circle. This is the seed of everything.

3. Domain, Codomain, and Periodicity

sin,cos:R→[−1,1] tan:R∖π/2+nπ∣n∈Z→R

Theorem (Periodicity). sin(θ+2π)=sinθ,cos(θ+2π)=cosθ∀θ∈R. tan(θ+π)=tanθ∀θ in the domain.

Proof sketch: Adding 2π to the arc parameter returns P(θ) to the same point on S1. □

Parity:

  • sin is odd: sin(−θ)=−sinθ
  • cos is even: cos(−θ)=cosθ
  • tan is odd: tan(−θ)=−tanθ

These follow immediately from the unit circle picture: reflecting (x,y) across the x-axis negates y but preserves x.

4. Fundamental Identities — A Derived, Not Memorized, Framework

4.1 Pythagorean Identity

Since P(θ)=(cosθ,sinθ) lies on x2+y2=1:

cos2θ+sin2θ=1​

Dividing by cos2θ: 1+tan2θ=sec2θ

Dividing by sin2θ: cot2θ+1=csc2θ

4.2 Angle Addition Formulas

sin(α+β)=sinαcosβ+cosαsinβ cos(α+β)=cosαcosβ−sinαsinβ

Derivation (via rotation matrices, MIT 18.02 style). A rotation by angle θ in R2 is the linear map Rθ​=(cosθ​−sinθ sinθ​cosθ​). Composing rotations: Rα​Rβ​=Rα+β​. Expanding the matrix product: Rα​Rβ​=(cosαcosβ−sinαsinβ​−(cosαsinβ+sinαcosβ) sinαcosβ+cosαsinβ​cosαcosβ−sinαsinβ​). Reading off (1,1) and (2,1) entries gives the addition formulas. □

This derivation is more satisfying than the geometric one — it shows that trig identities are really statements about linear algebra.

4.3 Double-Angle Formulas

Set β=α: sin(2θ)=2sinθcosθ cos(2θ)=cos2θ−sin2θ=1−2sin2θ=2cos2θ−1

4.4 Half-Angle / Power-Reduction Formulas

From cos(2θ)=1−2sin2θ: sin2θ=21−cos(2θ)​,cos2θ=21+cos(2θ)​

Why this matters: These are the engine of trigonometric integration. Any power sinnθ or cosnθ can be reduced to lower powers using these repeatedly.

4.5 Product-to-Sum Formulas

sinαcosβ=21​[sin(α+β)+sin(α−β)] cosαcosβ=21​[cos(α−β)+cos(α+β)] sinαsinβ=21​[cos(α−β)−cos(α+β)]

Key Geometric Observations

1. Phase Relationship: cosθ=sin!(θ+2π​) Cosine is sine shifted left by π/2. They are the same wave, just out of phase.

2. Amplitude, Period, Phase Shift. For the general sinusoid: f(θ)=Asin(Bθ+C)+D

  • Amplitude: ∣A∣ — vertical stretch
  • Period: 2π/∣B∣ — horizontal stretch
  • Phase shift: −C/B — horizontal translation
  • Vertical shift: D

Example. f(θ)=3sin(2θ−π/4)+1:

  • Amplitude =3, Period =π, Phase shift =π/8 (right), Vertical shift =1.

3. Tangent Graph. tanθ has vertical asymptotes at θ=π/2+nπ and period π. Between consecutive asymptotes, tan is strictly increasing from −∞ to +∞.

To invert sin,cos,tan, we must restrict domains to make them injective.

Careful! sin−1 notation is ambiguous. In this note I’ll write arcsin.

Key identity: arcsin(x)+arccos(x)=2π​∀x∈[−1,1]. Proof: If arcsinx=θ, then sinθ=x, so cos(π/2−θ)=x, so arccosx=π/2−θ. □

Useful limit: limx→±∞​arctan(x)=±2π​. arctan has horizontal asymptotes at y=±π/2 — important in ML (sigmoid is related to arctan).

7. Limits Involving Trig Functions

The Fundamental Limit

limθ→0​θsinθ​=1

Rigorous proof (Squeeze Theorem).

For 0<θ<π/2, compare areas:

  • Area of triangle OAP (inner) =21​sinθ
  • Area of circular sector OAP =21​θ
  • Area of triangle OAT (outer) =21​tanθ

So: 21​sinθ≤21​θ≤21​tanθ

Dividing by 21​sinθ>0: 1≤sinθθ​≤cosθ1​

Taking reciprocals (reversing inequalities): cosθ≤θsinθ​≤1

Since cosθ→1 as θ→0, the Squeeze Theorem gives θ→0lim​θsinθ​=1. □

The case θ→0− follows by the odd symmetry of sinθ/θ.

Corollary

limθ→0​θ1−cosθ​=0

Proof: θ1−cosθ​=θ(1+cosθ)(1−cosθ)(1+cosθ)​=θ(1+cosθ)sin2θ​=θsinθ​⋅1+cosθsinθ​→1⋅0=0.□

9. Integrals

9.1 Basic Antiderivatives

∫sinθ,dθ=−cosθ+C,∫cosθ,dθ=sinθ+C ∫tanθ,dθ=−ln∣cosθ∣+C=ln∣secθ∣+C ∫sec2θ,dθ=tanθ+C,∫csc2θ,dθ=−cotθ+C

Derivation of ∫tanθ,dθ: ∫cosθsinθ​,dθ=−∫cosθ−sinθ​,dθ=−ln∣cosθ∣+C. (Substitution: u=cosθ, du=−sinθ,dθ.) □

9.2 Powers of Sine and Cosine

Strategy for ∫sinnθ,dθ:

Case 1: n odd. Factor off sinθ, convert sinn−1θ=(1−cos2θ)(n−1)/2, substitute u=cosθ.

Example: ∫sin3θ,dθ=∫sin2θ⋅sinθ,dθ=∫(1−cos2θ)sinθ,dθ Let u=cosθ: =−∫(1−u2),du=−u+3u3​+C=−cosθ+3cos3θ​+C.

Case 2: n even. Use power-reduction: sin2θ=21−cos(2θ)​.

Example: ∫sin2θ,dθ=∫21−cos(2θ)​,dθ=2θ​−4sin(2θ)​+C.

9.3 Important Integral

∫−ππ​sin(mθ)sin(nθ),dθ={0​m=n π​m=n​

This is the orthogonality of the Fourier basis — foundational for Fourier analysis, signal processing, and attention mechanisms in transformers. See §12.

10. Taylor / Maclaurin Series

Using the Maclaurin series (Taylor series centered at 0):

sinx=∑n=0∞​(2n+1)!(−1)nx2n+1​=x−6x3​+120x5​−⋯

cosx=∑n=0∞​(2n)!(−1)nx2n​=1−2x2​+24x4​−⋯

Both series converge for all x∈R (radius of convergence =∞, by the ratio test).

Immediate consequences:

  • x→0lim​xsinx​=x→0lim​(1−6x2​+⋯)=1 ✓
  • For small x: sinx≈x (first-order approximation), cosx≈1−x2/2 (second-order).

ML relevance: The small-angle approximation sinθ≈θ shows up in linearization of neural ODE flows and in the analysis of gradient descent near fixed points.

11. Complex Exponential — The Unifying Perspective

Euler’s Formula

eiθ=cosθ+isinθ​

Proof via Taylor series. Expand eiθ: eiθ=∑n=0∞​n!(iθ)n​=∑k=0∞​(2k)!(iθ)2k​+∑k=0∞​(2k+1)!(iθ)2k+1​. Using i2k=(−1)k and i2k+1=i(−1)k: =∑k=0∞​(2k)!(−1)kθ2k​+i∑k=0∞​(2k+1)!(−1)kθ2k+1​=cosθ+isinθ.□

Consequences

cosθ=2eiθ+e−iθ​,sinθ=2ieiθ−e−iθ​

These are the inverse Euler formulas. They make all trig identities trivial — they reduce to algebra with exponentials.

Example. Prove cos2θ−sin2θ=cos(2θ): cos2θ−sin2θ=(2eiθ+e−iθ​)2−(2ieiθ−e−iθ​)2 =4e2iθ+2+e−2iθ​+4e2iθ−2+e−2iθ​=2e2iθ+e−2iθ​=cos(2θ).□

Euler’s Identity

Setting θ=π: eiπ+1=0 Five of the most fundamental constants in mathematics in one equation.

12. Connections to AI / Machine Learning / Deep Learning

This section is the payoff. Trigonometric functions appear everywhere in modern ML.

12.1 Activation Functions: Sigmoid and Tanh

The sigmoid function: σ(x)=1+e−x1​

The hyperbolic tangent: tanh(x)=ex+e−xex−e−x​

These are directly related to trig via hyperbolic functions (replace iθ with real θ in Euler’s formula): cos(ix)=cosh(x),sin(ix)=isinh(x).

The connection to sigmoid: σ(x)=21​(1+tanh(2x​)).

Properties inherited from trig:

  • tanh is odd: tanh(−x)=−tanh(x), just like tan and sin
  • tanh′(x)=1−tanh2(x) — analogous to sec2=1+tan2
  • Both saturate (output bounded), causing the vanishing gradient problem

Insight: The reason tanh saturates is the same reason sin flattens near ±π/2: these are transcendental functions with bounded range. In the early 2010s this motivated the switch to ReLU.

12.2 Fourier Analysis and Spectral Methods

The Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) expresses any signal x0​,…,xN−1​ as: Xk​=∑n=0N−1​xn​,e−2πikn/N=∑n=0N−1​xn​[cos!(N2πkn​)−isin!(N2πkn​)].

This is a change of basis from the standard basis to the Fourier basis e2πikn/Nk=0N−1​, which is an orthonormal basis by the integral orthogonality of §9.3.

ML Applications:

  • Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs): Convolution in the time/spatial domain = pointwise multiplication in the Fourier domain. CNNs implicitly learn frequency filters.
  • Spectral Graph Neural Networks: Graph convolutions are defined via the graph Laplacian’s eigenvectors, which generalize Fourier modes to non-Euclidean domains.
  • FNO (Fourier Neural Operator): Solves PDEs by learning in Fourier space — directly uses sin and cos as basis functions.

12.3 Positional Encoding in Transformers

This is arguably the most striking direct appearance of trig in modern deep learning.

The Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) needs to encode the position of each token in a sequence. The positional encoding for position pos and dimension i is:

PE(pos,2i)=sin!(100002i/dmodel​pos​) PE(pos,2i+1)=cos!(100002i/dmodel​pos​)

Why sinusoids?

  1. Uniqueness: Each position gets a unique encoding (sinusoids at different frequencies create a unique “fingerprint”).
  2. Relative position: For any fixed offset k, the encoding of position pos+k is a linear transformation of the encoding of position pos. This follows from the angle addition formula: sin(pos+k)ω=sin(pos⋅ω)cos(kω)+cos(pos⋅ω)sin(kω). The model can learn to attend to relative positions using a fixed linear map — this is only possible because of the addition formula.
  3. Generalization: Sinusoids extrapolate — the model sees longer sequences than it was trained on.

Personal note: The fact that the attention mechanism can represent relative positions because of a 2000-year-old identity (sin(α+β) addition formula) is one of the most beautiful connections between classical math and modern AI I know of.

12.4 Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)

Used in LLaMA, GPT-NeoX, and many modern LLMs. The key idea: encode position by rotating query and key vectors in embedding space.

For a 2D subspace, position m applies the rotation matrix: Rm​=(cos(mθ)​−sin(mθ) sin(mθ)​cos(mθ)​)

The dot product of query at position m and key at position n depends only on m−n: ⟨Rm​q,,Rn​k⟩=⟨Rm−n​q,,k⟩.

This is exactly the rotation-matrix composition property from MIT 18.02: Rm⊤​Rn​=Rn−m​. Positional information is encoded in the angle between vectors in the attention computation.

12.5 Random Fourier Features (Kernel Methods)

The RBF (Gaussian) kernel can be approximated using random sinusoids (Rahimi & Recht, 2007): k(x,y)=e−∣x−y∣2/2σ2≈D1​∑j=1D​cos(ωj⊤​x+bj​)cos(ωj⊤​y+bj​) where ωj​∼N(0,I/σ2) and bj​∼Uniform(0,2π).

This is Bochner’s theorem: every positive definite kernel is the Fourier transform of a non-negative measure. Trig functions are the bridge between kernel methods and explicit feature maps.

12.6 Periodic Activations — SIREN Networks

SIREN (Sinusoidal Representation Networks, Sitzmann et al., 2020) use sin as the activation function: ϕi​(x)=sin(Wi​x+bi​)

Why?

  • dxd​sin(ωx)=ωcos(ωx) — derivatives are also sinusoids. A SIREN’s gradient network is itself a SIREN.
  • Periodic activations represent high-frequency signals (images, audio, physical simulations) far more compactly than ReLU networks.
  • The Taylor series of sin has all odd-degree terms — SIREN naturally represents odd/symmetric functions.

The initialization scheme sets weights so that Wi​x∼Uniform(−π,π), ensuring the sine inputs span a full period.

12.7 Signal Processing in Audio/Vision Deep Learning

CNNs for audio (WaveNet, Wav2Vec) implicitly perform short-time Fourier transforms. Mel-spectrograms — the standard audio feature — are: S(m,t)=​∑n​x(n),e−2πi,mn/N​2 (filtered through mel filterbank)

Every spectrogram you feed to a speech recognition model is a visualization of trig functions applied to the signal.
