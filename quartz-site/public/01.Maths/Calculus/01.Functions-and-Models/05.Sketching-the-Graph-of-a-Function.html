1. The Philosophy of Curve Sketching

Before computers, sketching was the way mathematicians understood functions. Even now, the discipline of extracting geometric information from f, f′, and f′′ is not obsolete — it is the exact skill that underlies:

  • Reading loss landscapes in optimization
  • Understanding activation function behavior
  • Analyzing convergence of iterative algorithms
  • Interpreting Hessian structure in second-order methods

The goal is not a photorealistic plot. The goal is a qualitatively correct skeleton that captures every geometrically meaningful feature.

3. Each Tool, Rigorously

A. Domain

The natural domain of f is the largest subset of R on which f(x) is defined.

Common restrictions:

  • g(x)​: require g(x)≥0
  • lng(x): require g(x)>0
  • g(x)1​: require g(x)=0
  • arcsing(x): require −1≤g(x)≤1

Personal note: Finding the domain first prevents you from computing derivatives at points where the function doesn’t even exist. This is where most computational errors begin.

B. Intercepts

y-intercept: Set x=0, compute f(0) (if 0 is in the domain).

x-intercepts (zeros): Solve f(x)=0. These are often hard to find analytically — note that they exist (by IVT) whenever f changes sign.

C. Symmetry

Definition 3.1. f is even if f(−x)=f(x) for all x in the domain. Its graph is symmetric about the y-axis.

Definition 3.2. f is odd if f(−x)=−f(x) for all x in the domain. Its graph has 180° rotational symmetry about the origin.

Definition 3.3. f is periodic with period T if f(x+T)=f(x) for all x.

Strategy: If f is even, sketch only x≥0 and reflect. If f is odd, sketch x≥0 and rotate.

D. Asymptotes

D.1 Horizontal Asymptotes

Definition 3.4. The line y=L is a horizontal asymptote of f if limx→+∞​f(x)=Lorlimx→−∞​f(x)=L

A function can have at most two horizontal asymptotes (one in each direction), and it can cross a horizontal asymptote for finite x.

D.2 Vertical Asymptotes

Definition 3.5. The line x=a is a vertical asymptote if any of the one-sided limits limx→a±​f(x)=±∞.

At a vertical asymptote x=a, determine the sign of f(x) from each side — the graph approaches +∞ or −∞ independently on the left and right.

D.3 Slant (Oblique) Asymptotes

If limx→∞​[f(x)−(mx+b)]=0 for some m=0, then y=mx+b is a slant asymptote.

How to find it: Perform polynomial long division when deg(numerator)=deg(denominator)+1. The quotient (ignoring the remainder) gives the slant asymptote.

f(x)=q(x)p(x)​long division​(mx+b)+q(x)r(x)​

As x→∞, the remainder q(x)r(x)​→0, so f(x)→mx+b.

E. Intervals of Increase and Decrease

Compute f′(x). Find critical numbers: f′(c)=0 or f′(c) DNE.

Build a sign chart for f′:

  • f′(x)>0⇒f↗ (increasing)
  • f′(x)<0⇒f↘ (decreasing)

(See Increasing and Decreasing Functions for the full rigorous treatment and MVT-based proof.)

F. Local Extrema

First Derivative Test

Theorem 3.1 (First Derivative Test). Let c be a critical number of f, and suppose f is continuous at c.

(a) If f′ changes from + to − at c: local maximum at c.
(b) If f′ changes from − to + at c: local minimum at c.
(c) If f′ does not change sign at c: no extremum (e.g., f(x)=x3 at c=0).

Second Derivative Test (when applicable)

Theorem 3.2 (Second Derivative Test). Suppose f′(c)=0 and f′′(c) exists.

(a) f′′(c)>0⇒ local minimum at c (concave up → “cup”).
(b) f′′(c)<0⇒ local maximum at c (concave down → “cap”).
(c) f′′(c)=0⇒ inconclusive — revert to First Derivative Test.

Proof sketch of (a): If f′′(c)>0, then f′ is increasing near c. Since f′(c)=0, f′<0 just left of c and f′>0 just right of c — exactly the condition for a local minimum by Theorem 3.1. □

G. Concavity and Inflection Points

Definition 3.6. f is concave up on I if f′ is increasing on I, equivalently f′′(x)>0 on I.
f is concave down on I if f′ is decreasing on I, equivalently f′′(x)<0 on I.

Geometric meaning:

  • Concave up: the graph lies above all its tangent lines — like a bowl ∪.
  • Concave down: the graph lies below all its tangent lines — like an arch ∩.

Definition 3.7 (Inflection Point). A point (c,f(c)) is an inflection point if f is continuous at c and concavity changes at c.

Warning: f′′(c)=0 is necessary but not sufficient. We need the sign of f′′ to actually change.

Counterexample: f(x)=x4. f′′(x)=12x2, so f′′(0)=0, but f′′≥0 everywhere — no sign change, no inflection.

Sign chart for f′′:

  • f′′(x)>0: concave up ∪
  • f′′(x)<0: concave down ∩
  • Sign change: inflection point

H. Sketch

With all the information assembled, plot in this order:

  1. Draw asymptotes as dashed lines.
  2. Plot intercepts and labeled extrema.
  3. Plot inflection points.
  4. Connect with curves, respecting:
    • Increasing/decreasing direction
    • Concavity (cup vs. arch)
    • Behavior near asymptotes (which side, +∞ or −∞)

4. The Full Algorithm in Action — Worked Examples

Example 4.1 — Rational Function with Slant Asymptote

f(x)=x−1x2​

A. Domain: x=1, so domain is (−∞,1)∪(1,∞).

B. Intercepts:

  • f(0)=0: y-intercept at (0,0).
  • f(x)=0⇒x2=0⇒x=0: x-intercept at (0,0).

C. Symmetry: f(−x)=−x−1x2​=f(x) and =−f(x). No symmetry.

D. Asymptotes:

Vertical: limx→1−​x−1x2​: numerator →1>0, denominator →0−, so limit =−∞.
limx→1+​x−1x2​=+∞.
Vertical asymptote: x=1.

Horizontal: limx→±∞​x−1x2​=±∞. No horizontal asymptote.

Slant: Long division: x−1x2​=x+1+x−11​ As x→±∞, x−11​→0. Slant asymptote: y=x+1.

E. Increase/Decrease:

f′(x)=(x−1)22x(x−1)−x2⋅1​=(x−1)2x2−2x​=(x−1)2x(x−2)​

Critical numbers: x=0 and x=2 (where f′=0); x=1 is not in domain.

Sign chart for f′:

(1,2)23​+−+−↘
(2,∞)3++++↗

F. Local Extrema:

  • x=0: f′ goes +→− → local max, f(0)=0.
  • x=2: f′ goes −→+ → local min, f(2)=14​=4.

G. Concavity:

f′′(x)=dxd​[(x−1)2x(x−2)​]

Using quotient rule: Numerator: (2x−2)(x−1)2−x(x−2)⋅2(x−1)=(x−1)[(2x−2)(x−1)−2x(x−2)]
=(x−1)[2x2−4x+2−2x2+4x]=(x−1)(2)=2(x−1)

f′′(x)=(x−1)42(x−1)​=(x−1)32​

Sign of f′′:

  • x<1: (x−1)3<0⇒f′′<0 → concave down ∩
  • x>1: (x−1)3>0⇒f′′>0 → concave up ∪

No inflection points (concavity changes at x=1, but x=1 is not in domain).

Example 4.2 — Classic Full Example

f(x)=x2+12x2​

A. Domain: All R (denominator never zero).

B. Intercepts: f(0)=0. f(x)=0⇒x=0. Only intercept: (0,0).

C. Symmetry: f(−x)=x2+12x2​=f(x). Even function — symmetric about y-axis. Sketch x≥0 only.

D. Asymptotes:

limx→±∞​x2+12x2​=limx→∞​1+1/x22​=2

Horizontal asymptote: y=2 (both sides). No vertical asymptotes.

E–F. Derivatives:

f′(x)=(x2+1)24x(x2+1)−2x2⋅2x​=(x2+1)24x​

  • f′(x)=0⇒x=0.
  • f′<0 for x<0 (decreasing), f′>0 for x>0 (increasing).
  • Local (and global) minimum at x=0: f(0)=0.

G. Concavity:

f′′(x)=(x2+1)44(x2+1)2−4x⋅2(x2+1)(2x)​=(x2+1)34(x2+1)−16x2​=(x2+1)34−12x2​

f′′(x)=0⇒4−12x2=0⇒x=±3​1​

  • ∣x∣<3​1​: f′′>0 → concave up ∪
  • ∣x∣>3​1​: f′′<0 → concave down ∩

Inflection points: (±3​1​,;f!(3​1​))=(±3​1​,;21​)

Example 4.3 — Transcendental Function

f(x)=xe−x

A. Domain: All R.

B. Intercepts: f(0)=0. f(x)=0⇒x=0.

C. Symmetry: Neither even nor odd.

D. Asymptotes:

limx→+∞​xe−x=0(exponential dominates polynomial) limx→−∞​xe−x=−∞(x→−∞,;e−x→+∞)

Horizontal asymptote: y=0 as x→+∞ only.

E–F.

f′(x)=e−x(1−x)

Critical number: x=1.

  • x<1: f′>0 → increasing
  • x>1: f′<0 → decreasing

Local (global) max at x=1: f(1)=e−1=1/e≈0.368.

G.

f′′(x)=−e−x(1−x)+e−x(−1)=e−x(x−2)

f′′(x)=0⇒x=2.

  • x<2: f′′<0 → concave down ∩
  • x>2: f′′>0 → concave up ∪

Inflection point at x=2: f(2)=2e−2≈0.271.

5.4 The Slant Asymptote Mechanism

f(x) = x + 1 + 1/(x-1)

f(x) for large x:
         /  ← f(x)
        /·
       / ·  ← gap = 1/(x-1) → 0
      /  ·
y=x+1─/─── ← slant asymptote

As x → ∞: gap = 1/(x-1) → 0.
The graph "hugs" the slant line from above (gap > 0 for x > 1).
For x < 1: gap = 1/(x-1) < 0, graph hugs from below.

6. Connections to AI, Machine Learning & Deep Learning

6.1 Loss Landscape Sketching — The Geometer’s View of Training

When you train a neural network, you are navigating the graph of a loss function L(θ). Everything in curve sketching maps directly:

Curve Sketching ConceptML Analogue
Local minimumConverged parameter state (good if loss is low)
Local maximumUnstable equilibrium — gradient descent flees
Saddle pointCritical point, ∇L=0, but not extremum
Inflection pointTransition from accelerating to decelerating loss decrease
Horizontal asymptoteIrreducible loss (Bayes error floor)
Concave up regionLoss decreasing at increasing rate → fast convergence zone
Concave down regionLoss decreasing at decreasing rate → convergence slowing

6.2 Activation Functions as Curves — Full Sketch Analysis

Every activation function’s behavior is determined by its graph. Here is the full curve-sketching analysis for the most important ones:

Sigmoid σ(x)=1+e−x1​

  • Domain: R
  • Range: (0,1)
  • Symmetry: σ(x)+σ(−x)=1 (antisymmetric about (0,0.5)) — odd after shifting
  • Asymptotes: y=0 as x→−∞; y=1 as x→+∞
  • f′(x)=σ(x)(1−σ(x))>0: always increasing
  • f′′(x)=σ′(x)(1−2σ(x)): zero when σ(x)=1/2⇒x=0
    • Inflection point at (0,0.5). Concave up for x<0, concave down for x>0.
  σ(x)↑
  1 ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ·····  ← y=1 asymptote
                 ····
             ····
  0.5       * ← inflection (0, 0.5)
         ····
     ····
  0 ─·····─────────────────→ x  ← y=0 asymptote
    (concave up)|(concave down)
                0

ML significance: The inflection at x=0 is where the neuron is most sensitive (f′ maximal). In the tails, f′≈0 → vanishing gradients. This is the geometric cause of the vanishing gradient problem.

ReLU R(x)=max(0,x)

  • Domain: R
  • Asymptotes: None
  • f′: 0 for x<0; 1 for x>0; undefined at x=0 (kink)
  • f′′: 0 everywhere except x=0 (distributional: δ(0))
  • No inflection points in the classical sense
  R(x)↑
       |              /
       |             /
       |            /
       |           /
       |          /   ← slope = 1 always (x > 0)
  ─────────────── ────→ x
       |    0
  (flat: f'=0)
  "dead neuron zone"

ML significance: Flat region (x<0) means f′=0 → zero gradient → weight doesn’t update → dead neuron. No concavity anywhere → ReLU networks are piecewise linear. They cannot model curvature within a single layer.

GELU ≈x⋅Φ(x) (where Φ is the Gaussian CDF)

  • Not monotone: has a local minimum near x≈−0.17
  • Smooth everywhere (unlike ReLU)
  • Inflection points exist; richer curvature structure
  • Used in: GPT-2, GPT-3, BERT, most modern transformers
  GELU(x)↑
         |               /
         |              /
         |             /
  ───────────────────── ────→ x
         |  ↙ slight dip
         | * ← local min ≈ (-0.17, -0.17)
         |
  (non-monotone near x = -0.17)

ML significance: The slight non-monotonicity allows GELU to “gate” inputs smoothly — small negative values are slightly suppressed, not hard-zeroed like ReLU.

6.3 The Hessian = f′′ in High Dimensions

In multivariable optimization, the Hessian H=∇2L is the matrix analogue of f′′:

1D curve sketchingnD optimization
f′′(c)>0 at critical pointH is positive definite at θ∗
f′′(c)<0 at critical pointH is negative definite
f′′(c)=0 (inconclusive)H is singular (degenerate — flat directions)
Inflection: concavity changesSaddle: H has both positive and negative eigenvalues

Newton’s method uses H−1 to adjust step size based on local curvature — exactly the second-order information that f′′ provides in 1D.

6.4 Learning Rate and the Geometry of the Loss

The shape of the loss graph directly constrains the safe learning rate η:

Safe step size: η<L2​

where L=maxθ​f′′(θ)= the Lipschitz constant of the gradient — essentially the maximum curvature of the loss curve.

Loss ℒ(θ)↑

  Highly curved region:         Flat region:
  small η needed                large η possible

        *                    ·  ·  ·  ·  ·
       * *                 ·                ·
      *   *              ·                    ·
     *     *                                   ·
    ← tight →           ←───── wide ─────────→

  f'' large here              f'' small here
  → L is large               → L is small
  → small η required         → large η safe

This is why learning rate scheduling and adaptive optimizers (Adam, RMSprop) are necessary: they implicitly adapt to local curvature.

6.5 Normalization and Asymptotic Behavior

The horizontal asymptote of an output function determines the range of a neuron’s output — critical for:

  • Sigmoid output layer: asymptotes at 0 and 1 → probabilities constrained to (0,1)
  • Tanh hidden layer: asymptotes at −1 and +1 → zero-centered outputs → faster convergence than sigmoid
  • Softmax: each output’s asymptote → proper probability simplex at temperature extremes
