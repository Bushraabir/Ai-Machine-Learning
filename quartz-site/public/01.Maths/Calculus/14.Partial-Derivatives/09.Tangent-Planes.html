09.Tangent Planes

09.Tangent Planes

Apr 23, 202610 min read

“A function is differentiable at a point if and only if it can be well-approximated by a linear map near that point. The tangent plane is that linear map, made geometric.”

§1. Motivation — From Tangent Lines to Tangent Planes

In single-variable calculus, given f:R→R differentiable at x=a, the tangent line is:

L(x)=f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)

This is the best linear approximation to f near a. It touches the curve and shares its slope.

Now let f:R2→R, i.e., z=f(x,y). The graph is a surface in R3. The natural question: what plays the role of the tangent line?

The answer is a plane — the tangent plane — and it is the unique plane that:

  1. Passes through the point (a,b,f(a,b)) on the surface
  2. Has the “correct” slope in every direction through that point

The existence of such a plane is not automatic. It requires differentiability, which in R2 is a strictly stronger condition than the existence of partial derivatives.

§2. Partial Derivatives — The Two Obvious Slopes

Fix a point (a,b) in the domain of f.

Definition. The partial derivatives of f at (a,b) are:

fx​(a,b)=limh→0​hf(a+h,,b)−f(a,b)​

fy​(a,b)=limh→0​kf(a,,b+k)−f(a,b)​

Geometrically: fx​ is the slope of the curve obtained by slicing the surface with the plane y=b; similarly fy​ with x=a.

Key warning. Existence of fx​ and fy​ does not guarantee differentiability. The classical counterexample:

f(x,y)={x2+y2xy​​(x,y)=(0,0) 0​(x,y)=(0,0)​

has fx​(0,0)=fy​(0,0)=0, but f is not even continuous at (0,0) (approach along y=x: limit is 21​). So no tangent plane exists there.

§3. Differentiability — The Right Definition

Definition (Stewart §14.4, MIT 18.02 Notes). f is differentiable at (a,b) if there exist constants A,B such that:

lim(h,k)→(0,0)​h2+k2​f(a+h,,b+k)−f(a,b)−Ah−Bk​=0

When this holds, one can show A=fx​(a,b) and B=fy​(a,b).

Interpretation. The error in approximating f(a+h,b+k) by f(a,b)+Ah+Bk vanishes faster than the distance h2+k2​ to the base point. This is exactly what it means for a linear map to be “tangent.”

Sufficient condition (very useful in practice). If fx​ and fy​ exist in a neighborhood of (a,b) and are continuous at (a,b), then f is differentiable at (a,b).

Most functions encountered in practice (C1 functions) satisfy this automatically.

§4. The Tangent Plane — Definition and Equation

Definition. If f is differentiable at (a,b), the tangent plane to the surface z=f(x,y) at the point P=(a,b,f(a,b)) is:

z=f(a,b)+fx​(a,b)(x−a)+fy​(a,b)(y−b)​

Why is this a plane? Rewrite as:

z−f(a,b)=fx​(a,b)(x−a)+fy​(a,b)(y−b)

This is of the form n⋅(r−r0​)=0 with normal vector n=⟨fx​(a,b),,fy​(a,b),,−1⟩.

Uniqueness. The tangent plane is the unique plane through P such that every curve on the surface through P has its tangent line lying in the plane. This is what “tangent” means geometrically for surfaces.

§5. The Linear Approximation (Linearization)

The tangent plane gives the linearization of f near (a,b):

L(x,y)=f(a,b)+fx​(a,b)(x−a)+fy​(a,b)(y−b)

For (x,y) near (a,b):

f(x,y)≈L(x,y)

The error is o(∣(x−a,y−b)∣), i.e., it goes to zero faster than the distance.

Total differential. Writing Δx=x−a, Δy=y−b, and Δz=f(x,y)−f(a,b):

Δz≈dz=fx​,dx+fy​,dy

This is the total differential — an infinitesimal version of the tangent plane approximation, and arguably the single most important formula in multivariable differential calculus.

§6. Worked Examples

Example 1 — Explicit Computation

Let f(x,y)=x2y+sin(πy). Find the tangent plane at (1,1).

Step 1. Evaluate: f(1,1)=1⋅1+sin(π)=1.

Step 2. Partial derivatives: fx​=2xy⟹fx​(1,1)=2 fy​=x2+πcos(πy)⟹fy​(1,1)=1+πcos(π)=1−π

Step 3. Tangent plane: z=1+2(x−1)+(1−π)(y−1)

Step 4. Check: at (1,1), z=1. ✓

Example 2 — Linearization for Approximation

Approximate f(x,y)=x2+y2​ near (3,4).

f(3,4)=9+16​=5.

fx​=x2+y2​x​⟹fx​(3,4)=53​ fy​=x2+y2​y​⟹fy​(3,4)=54​

Linearization: L(x,y)=5+53​(x−3)+54​(y−4)

Approximate f(3.02,3.97): L(3.02,3.97)=5+53​(0.02)+54​(−0.03)=5+0.012−0.024=4.988

Exact: (3.02)2+(3.97)2​=9.1204+15.7609​=24.8813​≈4.988. Very close. ✓

Example 3 — Error Estimation via Total Differential

A cylindrical can has radius r=5,cm and height h=10,cm, with measurement errors dr=0.05,cm, dh=0.1,cm. Estimate the error in the volume V=πr2h.

dV=∂r∂V​,dr+∂h∂V​,dh=2πrh,dr+πr2,dh =2π(5)(10)(0.05)+π(25)(0.1)=5π+2.5π=7.5π≈23.6,cm3

Think of it this way: place a flat board tangent to a curved hill at a point P. The board sits flush against the hill at P — touching it, matching the slope in both the east-west and north-south directions. That board is the tangent plane.

The normal vector to the plane is n=⟨fx​(a,b),,fy​(a,b),,−1⟩. It points “away” from the surface in the direction of steepest local tilt.

Implicit surfaces. For a surface defined by F(x,y,z)=0, the tangent plane at (a,b,c) is:

Fx​(a,b,c)(x−a)+Fy​(a,b,c)(y−b)+Fz​(a,b,c)(z−c)=0

with normal ∇F=⟨Fx​,Fy​,Fz​⟩. This is the gradient theorem: the gradient is always normal to level sets/surfaces.

§8. Connection to the Gradient and Directional Derivatives

The tangent plane formula can be written as:

z−f(a,b)=∇f(a,b)⋅⟨x−a,,y−b⟩

where ∇f=⟨fx​,fy​⟩ is the gradient.

The directional derivative in direction u^ is:

Du^​f=∇f⋅u^

This is the slope of the tangent plane in direction u^. The tangent plane encodes all directional derivatives simultaneously — it is the gradient’s geometric incarnation.

Maximum slope occurs in direction u^=∣∇f∣∇f​, with value ∣∇f∣.

§9. Connection to AI, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning

This is where the tangent plane stops being pure mathematics and becomes the engine of modern AI.

9.1 — Loss Landscapes and Local Linearization

In machine learning, we have a loss function L:Rn→R where n can be billions (the number of parameters θ of a neural network). The tangent plane at any point θ0​ in parameter space is:

L(θ)≈L(θ0​)+∇θ​L(θ0​)⊤(θ−θ0​)

This is exactly the n-dimensional tangent hyperplane. Gradient descent moves along this plane to find the steepest downhill direction:

θt+1​=θt​−η,∇θ​L(θt​)

Every gradient descent step is an implicit invocation of the tangent plane: we linearize the loss locally and take a step in the direction of steepest descent on that linearization.

9.2 — Backpropagation is Repeated Chain Rule on Tangent Planes

Consider a neural network f=fL​∘fL−1​∘⋯∘f1​ where each fi​ is a layer. Backpropagation computes ∂θ∂L​ via the chain rule:

∂θi​∂L​=∂fL​∂L​⋅∂fL−1​∂fL​​⋯∂fi​∂fi+1​​⋅∂θi​∂fi​​

Each factor ∂fk−1​∂fk​​ is a Jacobian matrix — the multivariable generalization of the derivative, which is precisely the linear map whose graph is the tangent plane (or tangent hyperplane) of fk​. Backpropagation is a recursive computation of tangent hyperplanes through the network.

9.3 — The Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK)

In the infinite-width limit, training a neural network is equivalent to kernel regression with the Neural Tangent Kernel:

K(x,x′)=∇θ​f(x;θ)⊤∇θ​f(x′;θ)

This kernel measures the inner product of the tangent vectors (gradients) of the output with respect to parameters. In this regime, the network dynamics are entirely governed by local linearization — the network behaves like its own tangent plane around initialization, and the loss evolves linearly. This is one of the deepest theoretical results in deep learning (Jacot et al., 2018).

9.4 — Second-Order Methods and the Hessian

The tangent plane is a first-order (linear) approximation. A second-order approximation uses the Hessian H=∇2L:

L(θ)≈L(θ0​)+∇L⊤δθ+21​δθ⊤H,δθ

This is a quadratic approximation — a paraboloid tangent to the loss surface. Methods like Newton’s method and natural gradient descent exploit this. The tangent plane is the zeroth-order piece of this expansion; understanding it is prerequisite to understanding curvature.

9.5 — Feature Space Geometry

In classification, a linear classifier defines a hyperplane (tangent-plane-like object) in feature space. Support Vector Machines find the optimal separating hyperplane. When the data is nonlinear, a kernel maps data to a high-dimensional space where it becomes linearly separable — one is finding a tangent plane in the lifted space. Deep networks can be understood as learning a feature map ϕ such that L(ϕ(x)) has a tractable tangent structure.

§11. Theorems to Know Cold

Theorem 1 (Differentiability ⇒ Continuity). If f is differentiable at (a,b), then f is continuous at (a,b).

Theorem 2 (Sufficient Conditions for Differentiability). If fx​ and fy​ exist in an open disk about (a,b) and are continuous at (a,b), then f is differentiable at (a,b).

Theorem 3 (Gradient and Level Curves). ∇f(a,b) is perpendicular to the level curve f(x,y)=c at (a,b).

Theorem 4 (Tangent Plane for Implicit Surfaces). If F(x,y,z)=0 defines a surface and F is differentiable with ∇F=0, the tangent plane at (a,b,c) is ∇F(a,b,c)⋅⟨x−a,y−b,z−c⟩=0.

Theorem 5 (Chain Rule, Gradient Form). If r(t)=(x(t),y(t)) is a smooth curve and f is differentiable: dtd​f(r(t))=∇f(r(t))⋅r′(t)

§12. Things That Will Trip You Up

Partial derivatives exist ⇒ Differentiable. The pathological example above (f=xy/(x2+y2) at origin) is the canonical counterexample. Always cite it on exams.

The tangent plane only makes sense when f is differentiable. Do not write the tangent plane formula for a point of non-differentiability.

The gradient ∇f∈R2, not R3. The normal to the tangent plane is ⟨fx​,fy​,−1⟩, which includes the −1 from the −z term.

Linearization = exact. L(x,y) is an approximation; the quality depends on second derivatives (curvature of the surface).
