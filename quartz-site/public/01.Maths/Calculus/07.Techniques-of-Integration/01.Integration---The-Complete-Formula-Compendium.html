§0 · The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Before any formula, the engine:

FTC Part I. If f is continuous on [a,b] and F(x)=∫ax​f(t),dt, then F′(x)=f(x).

FTC Part II. If F is any antiderivative of f on [a,b], then

∫ab​f(x),dx=F(b)−F(a)

Why it matters: every single formula below is just “find F such that F′=f.” The FTC is what makes the list below useful.

§6 · Integration by Substitution (u-sub)

Theorem. If g is differentiable and f is continuous: ∫f(g(x)),g′(x),dx=∫f(u),duwhere u=g(x)

This is the chain rule reversed.

Recognition pattern: look for a function and its derivative together.

Examples:

∫2x,ex2,dxu=x2​∫eu,du=ex2+C

∫1−x2​x​,dxu=1−x2​−21​∫u−1/2,du=−1−x2​+C

∫0π/2​sin3xcosx,dxu=sinx​∫01​u3,du=41​

AI/ML Connection 🤖: The change of variables formula in probability — pY​(y)=pX​(g−1(y))⋅∣(g−1)′(y)∣ — is the probabilistic cousin of u-substitution. This underpins normalizing flows in generative modelling (RealNVP, Glow), where we learn invertible transformations and must track the Jacobian determinant — the multivariable version of g′(x).

§7 · Integration by Parts

Theorem (reverse product rule): ∫u,dv=uv−∫v,du

LIATE mnemonic for choosing u:

Logarithm · Inverse trig · Algebraic · Trigonometric · Exponential

Canonical examples:

∫xex,dx=xex−ex+C=ex(x−1)+C

∫x2ex,dx=ex(x2−2x+2)+C(apply IBP twice)

∫xlnx,dx=2x2​lnx−4x2​+C

∫exsinx,dx=2ex(sinx−cosx)​+C(cyclic IBP)

Proof of cyclic IBP for ∫exsinx,dx ⚑: Let I=∫exsinx,dx. IBP with u=sinx, dv=exdx: I=exsinx−∫excosx,dx IBP again with u=cosx, dv=exdx: I=exsinx−[excosx+∫exsinx,dx]=exsinx−excosx−I 2I=ex(sinx−cosx),I=2ex(sinx−cosx)​+C

Reduction formulae (very useful):

∫sinnx,dx=−nsinn−1xcosx​+nn−1​∫sinn−2x,dx

∫cosnx,dx=ncosn−1xsinx​+nn−1​∫cosn−2x,dx

∫xnex,dx=xnex−n∫xn−1ex,dx

AI/ML Connection 🤖: IBP is the engine behind backpropagation in the continuous limit. The adjoint method used in Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) and physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) derives the gradient of a loss through an ODE solution using the integration-by-parts identity on the relevant operator. Also, computing Fisher information I(θ)=−E[∂θ2∂2​lnp(x;θ)] requires IBP to relate second-order terms to first-order ones.

§9 · Partial Fractions

Setup: For a rational function P(x)/Q(x) with degP<degQ, factor Q and decompose:

Case 1 — Distinct linear factors: (x−a)(x−b)1​=x−aA​+x−bB​

Case 2 — Repeated linear factors: (x−a)nP(x)​=x−aA1​​+(x−a)2A2​​+⋯+(x−a)nAn​​

Case 3 — Irreducible quadratic factors: x2+bx+cP(x)​=x2+bx+cAx+B​

Worked example (complete): ∫x2−x−22x+1​,dx=∫(x−2)(x+1)2x+1​,dx Partial fractions: (x−2)(x+1)2x+1​=x−2A​+x+1B​

Multiply through: 2x+1=A(x+1)+B(x−2)

Set x=2: 5=3A⇒A=5/3. Set x=−1: −1=−3B⇒B=1/3.

∫x−25/3​,dx+∫x+11/3​,dx=35​ln∣x−2∣+31​ln∣x+1∣+C

AI/ML Connection 🤖: Partial fractions appear in the z-transform and Laplace transform, used extensively in signal processing for audio/speech models and in analysing the stability of recurrent neural networks (RNNs). The transfer function of an RNN or an LSTM filter bank is a rational function in z or s, and partial fraction decomposition reveals the poles — i.e., the time constants of memory decay.

Type I (infinite limits): ∫a∞​f(x),dx=limt→∞​∫at​f(x),dx

Type II (unbounded integrand): ∫ab​f(x),dx=limt→a+​∫tb​f(x),dxif f unbounded near a

Key convergence tests:

p-integral: ∫1∞​xpdx​={p−11​​p>1 diverges​p≤1​

∫01​xpdx​={1−p1​​p<1 diverges​p≥1​

Gaussian integral ⚑ (MIT 18.02 — polar coordinates): ∫−∞∞​e−x2,dx=π​

Proof sketch: Let I=∫−∞∞​e−x2,dx. Then: I2=∫−∞∞​∫−∞∞​e−(x2+y2),dx,dy Convert to polar x=rcosθ, y=rsinθ, dx,dy=r,dr,dθ: I2=∫02π​∫0∞​e−r2r,dr,dθ=2π⋅21​=π Therefore I=π​.

This follows from the Gaussian integral above with substitution t=(x−μ)/(σ2​).

AI/ML Connection 🤖: Improper integrals are everywhere in ML:

  • Expected loss: E[L(θ)]=∫L(x,θ)p(x),dx over all data x∈Rd
  • Entropy: H(p)=−∫p(x)lnp(x),dx
  • ELBO in VAEs: L(ϕ,θ)=Eqϕ​(z∣x)​[lnpθ​(x∣z)]−DKL​(qϕ​(z∣x)∣p(z)) — both terms are improper integrals over Rd
  • Score matching: ∫∣∇x​lnpθ​(x)−∇x​lnpdata​(x)∣2pdata​(x),dx — this is what diffusion models (DDPM, Score-based generative models) minimise

§13 · Multivariable Integrals (MIT 18.02)

Double Integrals

Rectangular region: ∬R​f(x,y),dA=∫ab​∫cd​f(x,y),dy,dx=∫cd​∫ab​f(x,y),dx,dy

(Fubini’s theorem — interchange valid when f continuous on closed rectangle.)

General region (Type I): ∬D​f,dA=∫ab​∫g1​(x)g2​(x)​f(x,y),dy,dx

Polar coordinates: x=rcosθ, y=rsinθ, dA=r,dr,dθ ∬R​f(x,y),dA=∫αβ​∫r1​(θ)r2​(θ)​f(rcosθ,rsinθ),r,dr,dθ

Triple Integrals

Cylindrical: (r,θ,z), dV=r,dr,dθ,dz ∭f,dV=∫∫∫f(rcosθ,rsinθ,z),r,dr,dθ,dz

Spherical: (ρ,θ,ϕ), dV=ρ2sinϕ,dρ,dθ,dϕ ∭f,dV=∫∫∫f(ρsinϕcosθ,ρsinϕsinθ,ρcosϕ),ρ2sinϕ,dρ,dθ,dϕ

Change of Variables (general): For T:(u,v)↦(x,y): ∬R​f(x,y),dA=∬S​f(x(u,v),y(u,v))​∂(u,v)∂(x,y)​​du,dv

where ​∂(u,v)∂(x,y)​​=∣detJ∣ is the Jacobian determinant.

AI/ML Connection 🤖 — Central to Deep Learning:

  • Multivariable integration underpins all of probability in Rn. Every joint distribution p(x1​,…,xn​) over a dataset, every marginalisation p(x1​)=∫⋯∫p(x1​,…,xn​),dx2​⋯dxn​ is a multiple integral.
  • The Jacobian ∣detJ∣ is exactly the term tracked in normalizing flows and diffusion models: when you transform data x through a neural network fθ​, the log-likelihood involves lnp(f−1(z))+ln∣detJf−1​∣.
  • Monte Carlo integration: For high-dimensional integrals (like ∫R1000​p(x),dx), deterministic quadrature is infeasible — hence Monte Carlo estimation Ep​[f]≈N1​∑f(xi​), which is how all deep learning expectations are computed.
  • Attention mechanism (Transformers): Attention(Q,K,V)=softmax(QKT/dk​​)V can be interpreted as a discretisation of an integral operator ∫K(x,y)v(y),dy — the kernel integral of v weighted by attention scores.

§14 · Line Integrals (MIT 18.02)

Scalar line integral: ∫C​f(x,y),ds=∫ab​f(r(t)),∣r′(t)∣,dt

Vector line integral (work integral): ∫C​F⋅dr=∫ab​F(r(t))⋅r′(t),dt=∫C​P,dx+Q,dy

Green’s Theorem: ∮C​P,dx+Q,dy=∬D​(∂x∂Q​−∂y∂P​)dA

AI/ML Connection 🤖: The gradient flow interpretation of neural network training uses line integrals. The total loss change along a training trajectory θ(t) from θ0​ to θ∗ is ∫0T​∇θ​L⋅θ˙,dt — a line integral along the optimisation path. This is why path-dependent phenomena like gradient vanishing/exploding in RNNs are properly understood using these tools. Stokes’ theorem (the 3D generalisation of Green’s) appears in theoretical analyses of diffusion processes underlying score-based generative models.

§15 · Surface Integrals (MIT 18.02)

Surface area element: dS=n,dS,dS=​∂u∂r​×∂v∂r​​du,dv

Flux integral: ∬S​F⋅dS=∬D​F(r(u,v))⋅(∂u∂r​×∂v∂r​)du,dv

Divergence Theorem (Gauss): ∬​S​F⋅dS=∭V​(∇⋅F),dV

Stokes’ Theorem: ∮∂S​F⋅dr=∬S​(∇×F)⋅dS

AI/ML Connection 🤖: The Divergence Theorem is the continuous analogue of conservation laws in physics-informed neural networks. Neural PDE solvers (e.g., Physics-Informed Neural Networks by Raissi et al.) solve PDEs whose weak forms involve surface and volume integrals. In geometric deep learning on 3D meshes or point clouds, integrals over surfaces are discretised and approximated using graph neural networks.

Strategy Selection Guide

Given ∫f(x)dx, ask in order:

1. Is it a basic form? → Use §1–§5 directly.

2. Can u-sub work? → Look for g(x) and g'(x) together.
   (Especially: e^{u}, ln, trig, rational with recognisable numerator)

3. Does it involve √(a²−x²), √(x²+a²), √(x²−a²)?
   → Trig substitution (§8)

4. Is it a product: polynomial × (exp/trig/ln)?
   → Integration by parts, LIATE (§7)

5. Is it a rational function P/Q?
   → Partial fractions (§9)
   → If quadratic denominator doesn't factor: complete the square (§11)

6. Does it involve sin^n or cos^n?
   → Even power: half-angle identity
   → Odd power: factor one, u-sub the rest

7. None of the above → combination of techniques, or look it up.

The Mean Value Theorem for integrals is the theoretical backbone of Monte Carlo integration — the sample mean approximates the integral.

§18 · The Big Picture — Integration in the ML Stack

LEVEL 0: Single-variable integrals (§1–§12)
   └─ Gaussian normalisation, activation function integrals,
      expected values over 1D distributions

LEVEL 1: Multivariable integrals (§13)
   └─ Joint distributions, marginalisation, normalising constants,
      Bayesian inference, partition functions Z = ∫e^{-E(x)/T}dx

LEVEL 2: Change of variables / Jacobians (§13 + §8)
   └─ Normalizing flows, VAEs, diffusion models,
      reparameterization trick, score matching

LEVEL 3: Line/surface integrals (§14–§15)
   └─ Physics-informed NNs, geometric deep learning,
      continuous-time gradient flows, Neural ODEs

LEVEL 4: Functional integrals (beyond this course)
   └─ Path integrals, Gaussian processes as limits,
      Bayesian nonparametrics, quantum ML

Every deep learning paper that writes E[⋅] is hiding an integral. The question is always: can we compute it exactly, or must we approximate? The formulas in this notebook tell you when exact computation is possible.
