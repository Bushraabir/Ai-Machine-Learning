13.Hyperbolic Functions

13.Hyperbolic Functions

Apr 17, 202612 min read

§ 0 — Motivation: Why Should You Care?

Before we define anything, a confession: the name “hyperbolic” is not an accident, and these are not just “the sinh/cosh things you memorize for an exam.” They appear naturally in:

  • The shape of a hanging cable (catenary): y=acosh(x/a)
  • Special relativity: Lorentz boosts are hyperbolic rotations
  • Solutions to the heat equation and wave equation
  • The backpropagation algorithm in deep learning (tanh is one of the most classical activation functions)
  • Geodesics on the hyperbolic plane (differential geometry)

They also make many integrals tractable that would otherwise require painful trigonometric substitutions.

§ 1 — Definitions: Exponential Heart

Everything comes from two combinations of ex:

sinhx=2ex−e−x​,coshx=2ex+e−x​​

Think of ex=coshx+sinhx as splitting the exponential into its even part (cosh) and odd part (sinh).

The remaining four follow exactly as in trigonometry:

tanhx=coshxsinhx​=ex+e−xex−e−x​

cschx=sinhx1​,sechx=coshx1​,cothx=tanhx1​

Personal note: I always re-derive tanh from scratch instead of memorizing it. If you know the two boxed formulas above, you know everything.

§ 2 — The Hyperbola Connection (Why “Hyperbolic”?)

Recall that (cosθ,sinθ) parametrizes the unit circle x2+y2=1.

Claim: (cosht,sinht) parametrizes the right branch of the unit hyperbola x2−y2=1.

Proof: cosh2t−sinh2t=(2et+e−t​)2−(2et−e−t​)2

=4(et+e−t)2−(et−e−t)2​=44ete−t​=44​=1□

This is the fundamental identity:

cosh2x−sinh2x=1​

The parameter t is twice the area of the hyperbolic sector (analogous to how θ/2 is the area of a circular sector). This geometric fact is why t is called the hyperbolic angle.

§ 5 — Derivatives

Theorem. The derivatives of the hyperbolic functions are:

dxd​sinhx=coshx,dxd​coshx=sinhx

Proof (both at once):

dxd​sinhx=dxd​(2ex−e−x​)=2ex+e−x​=coshx

dxd​coshx=dxd​(2ex+e−x​)=2ex−e−x​=sinhx□

Contrast with trig: dxd​cosx=−sinx, but dxd​coshx=+sinhx. The minus sign is missing — this is the same sign twist from Osborn’s Rule.

The full table:

f(x)f′(x)
sinhxcoshx
coshxsinhx
tanhxsech2x
cschx−cschxcothx
sechx−sechxtanhx
cothx−csch2x

Proof for tanhx (quotient rule):

dxd​tanhx=cosh2xcoshx⋅coshx−sinhx⋅sinhx​=cosh2xcosh2x−sinh2x​=cosh2x1​=sech2x□

§ 6 — Inverse Hyperbolic Functions

Since sinh and tanh are strictly monotone on R, and cosh is monotone on [0,∞), the inverses exist.

Crucial fact: The inverses can be expressed in closed form via logarithms:

sinh−1x=ln!(x+x2+1​),x∈R

cosh−1x=ln!(x+x2−1​),x≥1

tanh−1x=21​ln!(1−x1+x​),∣x∣<1

Proof of the sinh−1 formula: Let y=sinh−1x, so sinhy=x:

2ey−e−y​=x⟹ey−e−y=2x⟹e2y−2xey−1=0

This is a quadratic in ey. By the quadratic formula:

ey=22x±4x2+4​​=x±x2+1​

Since ey>0 and x2+1​>∣x∣, we must take the + sign:

y=ln!(x+x2+1​)□

Derivatives of Inverses

dxd​sinh−1x=x2+1​1​,dxd​cosh−1x=x2−1​1​,dxd​tanh−1x=1−x21​

Proof of the first (implicit differentiation): If sinhy=x, then coshy⋅dxdy​=1, so

dxdy​=coshy1​=1+sinh2y​1​=1+x2​1​□

§ 7 — Integration Formulas

The derivatives immediately give antiderivatives:

∫coshx,dx=sinhx+C,∫sinhx,dx=coshx+C

∫sech2x,dx=tanhx+C

The real power comes from recognizing when a substitution reduces to these:

∫x2+a2​dx​=sinh−1!(ax​)+C=ln!(x+x2+a2​)+C

∫x2−a2​dx​=cosh−1!(ax​)+C(x>a>0)

∫a2−x2dx​=a1​tanh−1!(ax​)+C(∣x∣<a)

Example: Hyperbolic Substitution

Evaluate ∫x2+1​,dx.

Let x=sinht, so dx=cosht,dt and x2+1​=sinh2t+1​=cosht.

∫cosht⋅cosht,dt=∫cosh2t,dt=∫21+cosh2t​,dt=2t​+4sinh2t​+C

Back-substituting (t=sinh−1x, sinh2t=2sinhtcosht=2xx2+1​):

=21​sinh−1x+2xx2+1​​+C

Compare the trigonometric route (x=tanθ) — it’s messier. Hyperbolic substitution is cleaner for x2+a2​ integrands.

§ 8 — Taylor Series and Asymptotic Behavior

From the Taylor series of ex:

coshx=∑n=0∞​(2n)!x2n​=1+2!x2​+4!x4​+⋯

sinhx=∑n=0∞​(2n+1)!x2n+1​=x+3!x3​+5!x5​+⋯

tanhx=x−3x3​+152x5​−⋯(∣x∣<π/2)

These are exactly the circular Taylor series but without the alternating signs (another instance of the Osborn sign flip).

Asymptotics: For large x:

sinhx≈coshx≈2ex​,tanhx≈1−2e−2x

§ 9 — Connection to AI, Machine Learning, Deep Learning

This is where the abstract becomes immediately applicable.

9.1 — tanh as an Activation Function

In a neural network, a neuron computes σ(w⊤x+b) where σ is the activation function. The tanh function was one of the first standard choices:

tanh(z)=ez+e−zez−e−z​∈(−1,1)

Why tanh?

  1. Zero-centered output. The output range (−1,1) means the mean activation is near 0. This matters because if activations are all positive (as with the sigmoid σ=1/(1+e−x)∈(0,1)), gradients in backpropagation always have the same sign, causing inefficient zig-zag updates.

  2. Saturating nonlinearity. It introduces the nonlinearity without which a deep network collapses to a linear map (∵ composition of linear maps is linear).

  3. Smooth and differentiable. Gradient-based optimization (SGD, Adam) requires dzd​tanhz=sech2z=1−tanh2z. Notice: if you’ve already computed tanh(z), the derivative costs almost nothing to compute — a single multiplication and subtraction. This is computationally elegant.

The vanishing gradient problem: sech2z≤1 and sech2(z)→0 as ∣z∣→∞. In deep networks, gradients are products of many such terms. When neurons saturate (large ∣z∣), sech2(z)≈0, and the gradient signal vanishes through the chain rule. This is one reason ReLU (max(0,x)) became popular — it doesn’t saturate for x>0.

Backpropagation chain rule:

∂L/∂w₁ = (∂L/∂a_n) · (∏ sech²(zᵢ)) · (∂a₁/∂w₁)
                          i=2..n

If n is large and neurons saturate → product → 0 (vanishing gradient)

9.2 — sinh−1 and Data Preprocessing

In some ML pipelines, the transformation x↦sinh−1(x)=ln(x+x2+1​) is used as a variance-stabilizing transform for heavy-tailed data. Unlike log(x) (defined only for x>0), sinh−1 is defined on all of R and behaves like ln(2x) for large x and like x near 0. It is used in financial data preprocessing where returns can be negative.

9.3 — cosh in the Loss Function Landscape

The log-cosh loss is a smooth approximation to the mean absolute error:

L(y,y^​)=∑i​lncosh(yi​−y^​i​)

Since lncosh(x)≈2x2​ for small x (MSE behavior) and ≈∣x∣−ln2 for large x (MAE behavior), it inherits robustness to outliers from MAE while remaining twice-differentiable everywhere. This matters for optimization since MSE is sensitive to outliers and Huber loss has a non-smooth kink at the transition point.

9.4 — tanh in Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTMs)

In Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997), both tanh and the sigmoid σ appear in the core update equations:

C~t​=tanh(Wc​⋅[ht−1​,xt​]+bc​)(candidate cell state) ht​=ot​⊙tanh(Ct​)(output/hidden state)

The role of tanh here: squash the cell state into (−1,1) before it becomes the hidden state. It keeps magnitudes bounded over long sequences, partially mitigating the exploding gradient problem.

§ 10 — The Catenary: A Classical Application

Problem. A flexible inextensible cable of uniform linear density ρ hangs under gravity between two supports. Find the shape.

Let the lowest point be the origin. Let T0​ be the tension there (horizontal). At arc length s from the lowest point, the tension T acts tangentially, and ρgs is the weight of the hanging segment. Force balance:

Tcosθ=T0​,Tsinθ=ρgs

Dividing: tanθ=T0​ρgs​. Since tanθ=y′ and s=∫0x​1+(y′)2​,dx′, differentiating both sides in x:

y′′=T0​ρg​1+(y′)2​

Let a=T0​/(ρg) and p=y′. Then p′=a1​1+p2​, which separates:

∫1+p2​dp​=∫adx​⟹sinh−1(p)=ax​+C

With p(0)=y′(0)=0⇒C=0. So y′=sinh(x/a). Integrating:

y=acosh!(ax​)+C​

The catenary. The constant C is fixed by the height at the lowest point.

§ 12 — Exercises

1. (Identity) Prove cosh(x+y)=coshxcoshy+sinhxsinhy directly from the exponential definitions.

2. (Derivative) Compute dxd​[tanh(lnx)] and simplify. (Hint: what is tanh(lnx) explicitly?)

3. (Integral) Evaluate ∫01​tanhx,dx. (Express as lncosh(1), then think about what that is numerically.)

4. (Inverse) Verify the formula tanh−1x=21​ln1−x1+x​ by showing tanh(tanh−1x)=x.

5. (Application) Show that the arc length of y=coshx from x=0 to x=a equals sinha.

6. (ML) The derivative of the sigmoid σ(z)=1+e−z1​ satisfies σ′(z)=σ(z)(1−σ(z)). Show that tanh(z)=2σ(2z)−1, and use this to express (tanhz)′ in terms of σ.

7. (Challenging) Prove that ∫sechx,dx=arctan(sinhx)+C=2arctan(ex)+C. (This result bridges hyperbolic and circular functions in a surprising way.)
