07.Indeterminate Forms & L'Hôpital's Rule

07.Indeterminate Forms & L'Hôpital's Rule

Apr 17, 202612 min read

“An indeterminate form is not a number — it is a competition. Two tendencies fight each other, and the limit is the outcome of that fight. L’Hôpital’s rule is the referee.” — Personal note, drawn from Stewart §4.4 and MIT 18.02

0. Motivation: Why “0/0” Is Not Zero

Consider the innocent-looking limit: limx→0​xsinx​

If you naively substitute x=0: you get 00​. This is not the number zero. It is not 1. It is not undefined in the sense of “no limit exists.” It is indeterminate — the symbol 00​ carries no information about the actual limiting value. The answer could be anything: 1, 5, ∞, or the limit may not exist.

Indeed, x→0lim​xsinx​=1, while x→0lim​x5x​=5, and x→0lim​xx2​=0 — all of the form 00​.

The form itself tells you nothing. That is the point.

1. The Complete Taxonomy of Indeterminate Forms

There are seven indeterminate forms. All others reduce to one of these.

Difference∞−∞Two infinities cancel — how much?

Memorize this distinction. The non-indeterminate forms require no tools — just logic.

2. L’Hôpital’s Rule: Statement and Proof Sketch

Theorem 2.1 (L’Hôpital’s Rule) (Stewart §4.4)

Suppose f and g are differentiable on an open interval containing a (except possibly at a itself), and g′(x)=0 near a. If limx→a​f(x)=0andlimx→a​g(x)=0 or limx→a​∣f(x)∣=∞andlimx→a​∣g(x)∣=∞ then: limx→a​g(x)f(x)​=limx→a​g′(x)f′(x)​ provided the right-hand limit exists (or is ±∞). The theorem holds equally for one-sided limits and for a=±∞.

The rule applies only to 00​ and ∞∞​ directly. All other indeterminate forms must first be algebraically converted to one of these two.

Proof Sketch (for 00​, a finite)

Extend f and g by setting f(a)=g(a)=0, making them continuous at a. Apply the Cauchy Mean Value Theorem: for any x=a, there exists c between x and a such that:

g(x)−g(a)f(x)−f(a)​=g′(c)f′(c)​

Since f(a)=g(a)=0, this gives g(x)f(x)​=g′(c)f′(c)​.

As x→a, c→a (it is squeezed between x and a), so: limx→a​g(x)f(x)​=limc→a​g′(c)f′(c)​=limx→a​g′(x)f′(x)​■

Key insight: L’Hôpital’s rule is not “differentiate the fraction.” It is “differentiate numerator and denominator separately and form a new fraction.” This is not the quotient rule.

3. Applying the Rule: The Two Direct Forms

3.1 The 00​ Form

Example 3.1. x→0lim​xsinx​

Check: sin0=0, 0=0. Form is 00​. ✓

limx→0​xsinx​=Hlimx→0​1cosx​=cos0=1​

Example 3.2. x→1lim​x−1x3−1​

Form 00​: =Hlimx→1​13x2​=3

(Cross-check: factor as (x−1)(x2+x+1)/(x−1)=x2+x+1→3. Consistent.)

Example 3.3 (Apply twice). x→0lim​x2ex−1−x​

Form 00​: =Hlimx→0​2xex−1​(00​ again)=Hlimx→0​2ex​=21​

This is deeply related to the Taylor expansion: ex=1+x+2x2​+⋯, so x2ex−1−x​→21​.

3.2 The ∞∞​ Form

Example 3.4. x→∞lim​xlnx​

Form ∞∞​: =Hlimx→∞​11/x​=limx→∞​x1​=0

Conclusion: logarithms grow slower than any positive power of x. This is a fundamental fact in analysis and algorithm theory.

Example 3.5. x→∞lim​xnex​ for any fixed n∈N

Apply L’Hôpital n times: xnex​→nxn−1ex​→n(n−1)xn−2ex​→⋯→n!ex​→∞

Conclusion: ex grows faster than any polynomial. Every time you differentiate, you get another ex on top — the exponential is inexhaustible.

Hierarchy of Growth Rates (from these examples)

lnx≪xa≪ex≪ex2as x→∞

for any a>0. This ordering is provable entirely via L’Hôpital applied to ∞∞​ forms.

4. Converting the Other Five Forms

The remaining five indeterminate forms must be rewritten into 00​ or ∞∞​ before applying the rule.

4.1 Form 0⋅∞: Rewrite as a Fraction

If limf=0 and limg=∞: f⋅g=1/gf​(00​)orf⋅g=1/fg​(∞∞​)

Choose whichever gives a cleaner derivative.

Example 4.1. x→0+lim​xlnx

Form 0⋅(−∞). Write xlnx=1/xlnx​, form ∞−∞​:

=Hlimx→0+​−1/x21/x​=limx→0+​−11/x⋅x2​=limx→0+​(−x)=0

So xlnx→0 as x→0+. The zero “wins” over the logarithm.

This means f(x)=xlnx extends continuously to f(0)=0 — a useful fact in entropy calculations.

4.2 Form ∞−∞: Combine into a Single Fraction

Example 4.2. x→0lim​(sinx1​−x1​)

Form ∞−∞. Combine: =limx→0​xsinxx−sinx​(00​)

=Hlimx→0​sinx+xcosx1−cosx​(00​)

=Hlimx→0​2cosx−xsinxsinx​=20​=0

4.3 Power Forms 00, 1∞, ∞0: Take the Logarithm

Strategy: If L=limf(x)g(x), then lnL=limg(x)lnf(x), which is a 0⋅∞ form — handled by §4.1.

Example 4.3 (1∞). x→0lim​(1+x)1/x

Let L=limx→0​(1+x)1/x. Then: lnL=limx→0​xln(1+x)​(00​)=Hlimx→0​11/(1+x)​=1

So lnL=1, hence L=e1=e. This is the definition of e as a limit.

Example 4.4 (00). x→0+lim​xx

lnL=limx→0+​xlnx=0(from Example 4.1)

So L=e0=1.

Example 4.5 (∞0). x→∞lim​x1/x

lnL=limx→∞​xlnx​=0(from Example 3.4)

So L=e0=1.

5. Critical Warnings and Common Errors

Warning 5.1: Check the Form First

L’Hôpital’s rule applies only when the limit is genuinely indeterminate. Never apply it otherwise.

Wrong: limx→0​x+2sinx+x​=???limx→0​1cosx+1​=2

The original limit: substitute x=0: 0+20+0​=20​=0. Not indeterminate. The correct answer is 0, not 2. L’Hôpital gave the wrong answer here.

Warning 5.2: Circular Reasoning for xsinx​

The proof that dxd​sinx=cosx uses limx→0​xsinx​=1. Using L’Hôpital to prove that same limit is circular. The limit limx→0​xsinx​=1 must be established geometrically first (squeeze theorem), then used to derive the derivative of sinx.

Warning 5.3: The New Limit Must Exist

The theorem states: apply L’Hôpital if limg′f′​ exists. If it does not exist, this does not mean the original limit fails to exist — the original might still converge.

Example 5.3. x→∞lim​xx+sinx​

Form ∞∞​. Attempting L’Hôpital: 11+cosx​, which oscillates — no limit. Yet the original: xx+sinx​=1+xsinx​→1. The original limit exists even though L’Hôpital fails. The rule’s hypothesis was not satisfied (the derivative ratio has no limit), so the rule simply does not apply — this is not a contradiction.

Warning 5.4: Don’t Loop Forever

Sometimes repeated application of L’Hôpital cycles back to the original form. Recognize this and use algebra instead.

Example 5.4. x→∞lim​ex−e−xex+e−x​

Applying L’Hôpital: ex+e−xex−e−x​ — the numerator and denominator have swapped. The rule will cycle indefinitely. Instead, divide numerator and denominator by ex: 1−e−2x1+e−2x​→1−01+0​=1

6. Visualization: The 00​ Race

Think of limx→0​g(x)f(x)​ with f(0)=g(0)=0 as asking: which function reaches zero faster?

Both start at 0 when x = 0.

f(x) = x²:    ●━━━━━━━━━━━━━━○   (reaches 0 quadratically — fast)
g(x) = x:     ●━━━━━━━━━━━━━━○   (reaches 0 linearly — slow)

Ratio x²/x = x → 0:   f reaches 0 much faster, so the ratio → 0.

f(x) = x:     ●━━━━━━━━━━━━━━○   (linear)
g(x) = x:     ●━━━━━━━━━━━━━━○   (same speed)

Ratio x/x = 1 → 1:    Dead heat.

f(x) = x:     ●━━━━━━━━━━━━━━○   (linear — slow)
g(x) = x³:   ●━━━━━━━━━━━━━━○   (cubic — fast)

Ratio x/x³ = 1/x² → ∞:  g reaches 0 far faster, ratio blows up.

L’Hôpital measures this “speed comparison” via the ratio of instantaneous rates at the collision point.

7. Connection to Taylor Series: The Deep Reason L’Hôpital Works

L’Hôpital’s rule is, at its core, a corollary of first-order Taylor approximation.

Near x=a: f(x)≈f(a)+f′(a)(x−a),g(x)≈g(a)+g′(a)(x−a)

If f(a)=g(a)=0: g(x)f(x)​≈g′(a)(x−a)f′(a)(x−a)​=g′(a)f′(a)​

This is exactly L’Hôpital evaluated at a. The (x−a) factors cancel, which is the algebraic reason the rule works.

When f′(a)=g′(a)=0 too: you need second-order terms — apply L’Hôpital again. Each application peels off one order of the Taylor expansion.

Example 7.1. x→0lim​x21−cosx​

Taylor: 1−cosx=2x2​−24x4​+⋯

x21−cosx​≈x2x2/2​=21​

Via L’Hôpital twice: 2xsinx​→2cosx​→21​. ✓

8. Connection to AI, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning

Indeterminate forms appear throughout the mathematics of ML — often invisibly, inside definitions and derivations that practitioners take for granted.

8.1 Entropy and the 0⋅ln0=0 Convention

In information theory, the entropy of a discrete distribution p=(p1​,…,pn​) is: H(p)=−∑i=1n​pi​lnpi​

When pi​=0, the term 0⋅ln0 is the indeterminate form 0⋅(−∞).

Using Example 4.1: limp→0+​plnp=0. So the convention 0ln0:=0 is not arbitrary — it is the exact limit, ensuring entropy is continuous at zero probabilities.

This matters in:

  • Cross-entropy loss: L=−∑i​yi​logp^​i​ — if the model outputs p^​i​=0 for a true class (yi​=1), the loss is ∞. This is why softmax outputs are numerically clipped or log-sum-exp stabilized.
  • KL divergence: DKL​(P∣Q)=∑i​Pi​lnQi​)Pi​​. The form 0/0 under the log requires the convention 0ln(0/0)=0.

8.2 Softmax and the ∞∞​ Form

The softmax function is: σi​(z)=∑j​ezj​ezi​​

When logits zi​→∞, this is the ∞∞​ form. L’Hôpital’s intuition (the dominant term “wins”) justifies the fact that softmax converges to a one-hot distribution as logits become extreme. More rigorously, after dividing numerator and denominator by emaxj​zj​, the form resolves — exactly the algebraic technique of §5.4.

Numerical overflow in softmax is a practical manifestation of ∞∞​: computing e700 overflows float32. The standard fix — subtracting maxzj​ — is precisely the algebraic manipulation that resolves the indeterminate form before evaluating numerically.

8.3 The 00​ Form in Gradient Derivations

Many activation function gradients are defined as limits that hide 00​ forms:

Sinc activation: sinc(x)=xsinx​, defined as 1 at x=0 by taking the limit (Example 3.1).

Derivative of ReLU at 0: ReLU(x)=max(0,x) is not differentiable at 0 — the left and right derivatives disagree (0 vs 1). In practice, subgradient methods assign 0 or 1 arbitrarily. The reason we cannot resolve this via L’Hôpital is that the limit limh→0​hReLU(h)−ReLU(0)​=limh→0+​hh​=1=limh→0−​h0​=0 — the one-sided limits disagree. Not an indeterminate form problem; a genuine discontinuity in the derivative.

8.4 L’Hôpital-Type Reasoning in Neural Scaling Laws

The empirical neural scaling laws (Kaplan et al., OpenAI) describe how loss L behaves as compute C→∞: L(C)∼C−α

Comparing growth rates of different architectures involves limits of the form C−βC−α​ as C→∞ — 00​ forms. L’Hôpital (equivalently, the growth hierarchy from §3) resolves these: the architecture with larger α dominates (loses slower = better). The mathematical structure is identical to Example 3.4.

8.5 The Gumbel-Softmax and 00

The Gumbel-Softmax trick (for differentiable discrete sampling) involves: yi​=∑j​e(logpj​+gj​)/τe(logpi​+gi​)/τ​

As temperature τ→0, this approaches a one-hot sample. The limiting form involves ez/τ as τ→0+ — an e∞=∞1 competition — and the analysis reduces to comparing exponential growth rates, exactly the ∞∞​ framework.
