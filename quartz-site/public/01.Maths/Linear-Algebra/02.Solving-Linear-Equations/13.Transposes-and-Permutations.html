Transposes

The transpose of A∈Rm×n is the matrix AT∈Rn×m defined by: (AT)ij​=Aji​

Rows become columns and columns become rows. If: A=(14​25​36​)∈R2×3 then: AT=​123​456​​∈R3×2

Algebraic Properties

For matrices A, B of compatible dimensions and scalar α:

  1. (AT)T=A
  2. (αA)T=αAT
  3. (A+B)T=AT+BT
  4. (AB)T=BTAT
  5. If A is invertible: (A−1)T=(AT)−1

  • Proof of (4) — the reversal rule. Let C=AB, so Cij​=∑k​Aik​Bkj​. Then (CT)ij​=Cji​=∑k​Ajk​Bki​=∑k​(BT)ik​(AT)kj​=(BTAT)ij​.

    Therefore (AB)T=BTAT. ■

    Note the reversal: same pattern as (AB)−1=B−1A−1. Both the inverse and the transpose reverse the order of products. This is not a coincidence — it reflects the categorical duality between a linear map and its adjoint/dual.

  • Proof of (5). We want to show (AT)−1=(A−1)T, i.e., (A−1)T⋅AT=I. (A−1)TAT=(AA−1)T=IT=I using property (4). ■ So (AT)−1=(A−1)T, which is often written A−T.

The Transpose and Inner Products

The transpose has a clean characterization in terms of the standard inner product on Rn. For x∈Rm, y∈Rn, A∈Rm×n: ⟨Ax,y⟩Rm​=⟨x,ATy⟩Rn​

Proof. ⟨Ax,y⟩ =(Ax)Ty=xTATy =⟨x,ATy⟩ This identity is fundamental. It says that AT is the adjoint of A with respect to the standard inner product. In the abstract language of linear maps between inner product spaces, the adjoint is defined by exactly this property. The transpose is just what the adjoint looks like when you choose the standard basis.

This identity is also why the normal equations for least squares are ATAx^=ATb: the “best” x is the one for which the residual b−Ax is orthogonal to the column space of A, which means AT(b−Ax)=0.

Symmetric Matrices

A is symmetric if AT=A, i.e., Aij​=Aji​ for all i,j.

Symmetric matrices are always square. Examples: (23​35​),​104​02−1​4−17​​ The most important source of symmetric matrices: if A is any rectangular matrix, then ATA and AAT are both symmetric and positive semidefinite.

Proof of symmetry. (ATA)T=AT(AT)T=ATA. ■ Products of symmetric matrices are generally not symmetric unless they commute: (AB)T=BTAT=BA=AB in general.

Skew-Symmetric Matrices

A is skew-symmetric (or antisymmetric) if AT=−A.

Every square matrix decomposes uniquely into a symmetric and a skew-symmetric part: A=symmetric2A+AT​​​+skew-symmetric2A−AT​​​ The diagonal entries of a skew-symmetric matrix are all zero (since aii​=−aii​ implies aii​=0).

Permutations

A permutation matrix P∈Rn×n is a matrix obtained by reordering the rows of the identity matrix In​.

Equivalently, P has exactly one 1 in each row and each column, and 0s everywhere else. For n=3, there are 3!=6 permutation matrices: I=​100​010​001​​,P12​=​010​100​001​​,P13​=​001​010​100​​ P23​=​100​001​010​​,P123​=​001​100​010​​,P132​=​010​001​100​​ where Pσ​ denotes the permutation matrix for the permutation σ.

Action of a Permutation Matrix

Left multiplication by P: permutes the rows of whatever follows. Right multiplication by P: permutes the columns of whatever precedes.

Concretely, if P is the permutation matrix for σ∈Sn​, then: (PA)ij​=Aσ−1(i),,j​ That is, row i of PA is row σ−1(i) of A. (The inverse appears because P puts row σ(i) of the identity into row i of P, so multiplying by P sends row σ−1(i) of A to row i.)

In practice, when we do row swaps during elimination, we are left-multiplying by elementary permutation matrices (transpositions).

Inverse and Transpose of a Permutation Matrix

For any permutation matrix P: P−1=PT That is, P is an orthogonal matrix: PTP=PPT=I.

Proof. The columns of P are distinct standard basis vectors eσ(1)​,eσ(2)​,…,eσ(n)​. Since these are orthonormal, PTP=I. ■

Alternatively: (PTP)ij​=∑k​(PT)ik​Pkj​=∑k​Pki​Pkj​. Since P has exactly one 1 per row and column, Pki​Pkj​=1 only when k=σ−1(i)=σ−1(j), which forces i=j. So PTP=I.

This means: to undo a row permutation, just transpose the permutation matrix. This is used all the time in PA=LU factorization — to solve Ax=b, first compute PA=LU, then solve LUx=Pb.

The Group Structure

The set of all n×n permutation matrices forms a group under matrix multiplication, isomorphic to the symmetric group Sn​. In particular:

  • The product of two permutation matrices is a permutation matrix.
  • The identity I is the identity permutation.
  • Every permutation matrix has a unique inverse, which is its transpose. The number of n×n permutation matrices is n!.

Determinant of a Permutation Matrix

The determinant of a permutation matrix Pσ​ is the sign of the permutation σ:

det(Pσ​)=sgn(σ)={+1​if σ is an even permutation −1​if σ is an odd permutation​

Even/odd. A permutation is even if it can be written as a product of an even number of transpositions (adjacent swaps), and odd otherwise. The parity is well-defined (this is a non-trivial fact from group theory).

This connects to the full determinant formula:

det(A)=∑σ∈Sn​​sgn(σ)∏i=1n​Ai,σ(i)​

This sum is over all n! permutations. Each term is a product of n entries, one from each row and column, with a sign determined by the parity of the permutation. For n=3 this gives 6 terms; for n=4 it gives 24.

LDU Decomposition and Symmetric Matrices

For a symmetric matrix A=AT, the LDU factorization has a special structure:

A=LDLT

where D is diagonal and L is unit lower triangular. This halves the storage and computation relative to general LU (Cholesky goes further, writing A=LLT when A≻0).

The key point: U=LT because AT=(LDLT)T=LDLT=A. Symmetry of A propagates to the factorization.

Putting It Together: PA=LDU

For a general square invertible matrix, the full factorization is:

PA=LDU

where:

  • P is a permutation matrix (records row swaps from partial pivoting)
  • L is unit lower triangular (stores the multipliers ℓij​)
  • D is diagonal (the pivots)
  • U is unit upper triangular

Often one writes PA=LU by absorbing D into U, producing upper triangular U with the pivots on the diagonal (not necessarily 1s).

The transpose gives:

(PA)T=(LU)T=UTLT

So ATPT=UTLT — the LU factorization of AT (with column permutation PT) involves UT as the lower triangular factor and LT as the upper triangular factor. This is how Cholesky arises: if A=AT and A≻0, then A=LLT. see more16.PA = LU
